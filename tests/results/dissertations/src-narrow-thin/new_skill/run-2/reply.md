## Sources

**Peer-reviewed articles.** The Journal of School Health is described as peer-reviewed on its association's page (ASHA). The Rural Educator says its research articles are peer-reviewed.

1. **Peer-reviewed article.** Israel, W., Mulitauopele, C., Ma, M., Levinson, A. H., Cikara, L., & Brooks-Russell, A. (2020). Adolescent health behaviors in schools with 4- versus 5-day school weeks. *Journal of School Health, 90*(10), 794–801. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.12941
   A survey of Colorado high school students comparing 4-day and 5-day schools. It found mixed results: more engagement and physical activity, but less sleep and more bullying. I could read only a summary of the abstract, which doesn't mention extracurriculars. The RAND report below says this study found four-day students more likely to take part in extracurriculars. That's secondhand, so check it in the full text.

2. **Peer-reviewed article.** Tomayko, E. J., Thompson, P. N., Smith, M. C., Gunter, K. B., & Schuna, J. M., Jr. (2021). Impact of reduced school exposure on adolescent health behaviors and food security: Evidence from 4‐day school weeks. *Journal of School Health, 91*(12), 1055–1063. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.13095 ([free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8595551/))
   Uses Oregon student survey data (grades 8 and 11) to compare health outcomes. It describes the off day as time with possibly less structure and supervision. It doesn't discuss band or extracurriculars. It's background on what students do with the fifth day.

3. **Peer-reviewed article.** I couldn't confirm which section of the journal it ran in. Hollingworth, L., & An, B. P. (2025). The use of a four-day school week to recruit and retain teachers in a rural district. *The Rural Educator, 46*(2), 1–14. https://doi.org/10.55533/2643-9662.1504
   A case study of one rural district that combines surveys of administrators, parents and teachers with retention data. It concludes the schedule didn't harm teacher, student or parent well-being. I read only the abstract. It's about teachers, not band.

**Research organization report**

4. **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five? Implementation and outcomes of the four-day school week* (Report RR-A373-1). RAND Corporation. [PDF](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA300/RRA373-1/RAND_RRA373-1.pdf)
   The largest study I found, with surveys and site visits across several states.
   - In the 12 four-day districts visited, most held sports practices on the off day. Some electives and clubs also met, "but the activities often depended on the adult leaders' schedules" (pp. 41–42).
   - In one district, parents said their children had band practice on the fifth day. It also found that high schoolers often used the fifth day for jobs (p. 46).
   - It's not peer-reviewed, but it's a large, well-documented study. Watch for this: band comes up only in passing.

**Thesis**

5. **Master's thesis, not peer reviewed.** Smelser, B. M. (2020). *Survey of the effects of a four-day school week on music programs* [Master's thesis, Southern Illinois University Edwardsville]. ProQuest Dissertations & Theses (28260289). [ProQuest page](https://www.proquest.com/openview/feff2c6c9fba2f3bfe065a5ef3247f07/1?pq-origsite=gscholar&cbl=18750&diss=y) (open access)
   This is the only source I found about music programs directly. It's an online survey of music teachers in four-day districts, and 75 completed it in fall 2020, during COVID.
   - 61.2% of the teachers who described their schedule said they saw their students every day (p. 31).
   - On students' musical skill, 46.7% said the effect was neutral, 37.3% negative or very negative, and 16% positive or very positive (p. 34).
   - On whether students stay in music, 60.6% said there was no change, 28.2% said students were less likely to stay, and 11.3% said more likely (p. 34).
   - On recruitment, 62% said the effect was neutral and 31% negative or very negative (pp. 34–35).
   - It covers all music ensembles, not only marching band.
   - The title page says "Music Education" but the abstract and ProQuest say "Music Programs."
   - Watch for this: it's a small, self-selected sample from one unusual school year, so treat it as teacher opinion, not measured outcomes.

## Questions the results raise

- Is what you lose with a four-day week the *rehearsal* (often after school, which can move to the off day) or the *band class period* during the school day?
- Does seeing students daily on longer days make up for the lost day?
- Who decides what happens on the off day? RAND says it depended on the adult leaders.
