**First, a fact check on the "40%" claim.** I couldn't find research behind it. The closest studies I found are two small trials by the same research group:

- [Willems, Sahin & Cook (2018)](https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/id/eprint/3188/), *International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism*: 13 women drank matcha, then walked briskly for 30 minutes. They burned more fat during the walk (0.35 vs. 0.31 g/min), which works out to about 13% more. The repository summary says the authors warned that matcha's effects "should not be overstated" for weight loss. I couldn't see that line in the full text, so check it.
- [Willems et al. (2020)](https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/id/eprint/5332/), *Journal of Dietary Supplements*: 12 women took matcha daily for three weeks. Fat burning during a 30-minute walk was reported as 35% higher, but the spread was huge (±47%).

Both studies measured fat burned **during exercise**, in about a dozen women each. Neither measured fat burned over a whole day or actual weight lost. "Burns 40% more fat" goes well beyond what either one found.
