## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Do students learn less?** A 2021 RAND study of Idaho, New Mexico and Oklahoma found that students on four-day weeks fell behind five-day peers in reading and math over time ([Kilburn et al., research organization report](https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/does-four-equal-five-implementation-and-outcomes-of-the-four-day-school-week/)). Other studies find small or no effects in rural districts. The disagreement is less about *whether* there are effects and more about *where and for whom*.
- **Does it actually save money?** Districts usually save about 0.4% to 2.5% of their budget, according to the Education Commission of the States as cited by [Education Week (news article, April 2026)](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/the-4-day-school-week-what-research-shows-about-the-alternative-schedule/2026/04). A Colorado study put savings at 1–3% of spending, mostly from transportation, food service and support-staff pay ([Bowser 2025, working paper, not yet peer reviewed](https://edworkingpapers.com/policy-practice-series/ai25-1208)).
- **Does it help keep teachers?** In the RAND study, district leaders believed it did. The same Colorado paper found no significant drop in turnover for teachers, principals or support staff.

**Major voices**
- **Paul Thompson's group at Oregon State.** Their national study found adoption is "often financially motivated" and mostly a small, rural-district phenomenon ([Thompson et al. 2021, peer-reviewed, *Education Finance and Policy*](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:tpr:edfpol:v:16:y:2021:i:4:p:558-583)).
- **RAND researchers, including Emily Morton.**
- **State education departments** in Missouri, Oregon and Oklahoma.
- **EdWorkingPapers at Brown's Annenberg Institute**, which publishes newer quasi-experimental studies.

**Patterns**
- Most research is state by state, so results are hard to generalize.
- Four-day schools average about 148 days a year versus 180, with days nearly an hour longer (Thompson et al.).
- Families and students report high satisfaction even where test scores dip (RAND).
- Growth: more than 2,100 schools in 26 states in 2024–25, up from about 1,600 in 24 states six years earlier (Education Week).

**Open questions**
- **Effects outside school.** A [Journalist's Resource roundup](https://journalistsresource.org/education/four-day-school-week-research/) reports findings on juvenile crime, bullying, food insecurity and child care. I only saw those studies described there, not in the original papers.
- **Spread beyond rural areas.** Whether results hold as suburban districts adopt it is still unsettled.
- **Who pays the shifted costs.** Little research looks at families or hourly staff such as bus drivers and cooks.
