## Sources

**Peer-reviewed articles.** I confirmed each journal through Crossref and OpenAlex, two scholarly databases. All three publisher pages blocked my tool, so I could only read the abstracts and haven't given links. Search the titles in your library database to get the full text.

- **Peer-reviewed article.** Israel, W., Mulitauopele, C., Ma, M., Levinson, A. H., Cikara, L., & Brooks-Russell, A. (2020). Adolescent health behaviors in schools with 4- versus 5-day school weeks. *Journal of School Health, 90*(10), 794–801.
  This study used a statewide survey of Colorado high school students. Four-day schools were linked to some positive outcomes, including better student engagement and more physical activity, and some negative ones, including more bullying and less sleep. The RAND report below says this study also found more participation in extracurricular activities. That detail isn't in the abstract, so I've only seen it secondhand.
- **Peer-reviewed article.** Thompson, P. N., Gunter, K., Schuna, J. M., Jr., & Tomayko, E. J. (2021). Are all four-day school weeks created equal? A national assessment of four-day school week policy adoption and implementation. *Education Finance and Policy, 16*(4), 558–583.
  This is a national dataset of four-day districts. Most adopted the schedule to save money, and most are small and rural. The day off is often Friday. School days run nearly an hour longer, and the year averages 148 days compared with 180 for five-day schools.
- **Peer-reviewed article.** Thompson, P. N., Tomayko, E. J., Gunter, K. B., & Schuna, J. (2022). Impacts of the four-day school week on high school achievement and educational engagement. *Education Economics, 30*(5), 527–539. It was published online in December 2021, so check which year your APA guide wants.
  This study followed Oregon students who started a four-day schedule in high school. They had lower 11th-grade math scores, more chronic absence, and lower on-time graduation. The abstract doesn't mention activities.

**Research organization reports.** I read both of these in full.

- **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five? Implementation and outcomes of the four-day school week* (RR-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://doi.org/10.7249/RR-A373-1
  The researchers surveyed families and students in several states and visited 12 four-day districts.
  - Most of those districts held sports practices on the off day. Some clubs and electives met then too, depending on the adult leaders' schedules, and no district provided transportation to off-day activities (p. 43).
  - In one district, parents said their children had band practice on the off day. High schoolers often used that day to work (p. 47).
  - Students in grades 7–12 had about 3.5 more hours of free time per week (p. 39).
  - Away football games pulled players, cheerleaders, and band members out of afternoon classes (p. 55).
- **Research organization report.** Day, E., Golfen, J., Shimmel, L., Trevino, S., Grant, S., Steinka-Fry, K., Cabrera, B., Hamilton, S., Martinez, S., & Tanner-Smith, E. (2025). *What research exists on four-day weeks in K-12 schools?* HEDCO Institute for Evidence-Based Educational Practice, University of Oregon. https://hedcoinstitute.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2025-08/four-day-school-week-scoping-hedco-2025.pdf
  This review maps 133 studies on four-day weeks. Most of them measure test scores and attendance. Of the studies, 79 didn't report what schools did on the off day at all. Among those that did, 12 mentioned club sports. Music isn't a category in the review.

**Policy brief**

- **Policy brief (legislative research organization).** National Conference of State Legislatures. (2023, June 28). *Four-day school week overview.* https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview
  It says the schedule gives students more time for extracurricular activities. It also says that when schools offer optional activities on the off day, a lack of transportation could limit who can attend.

## Questions these results raise

- Your position assumes band rehearses during the school day. Several sources describe band and other activities moving to the off day or to longer school days instead.
- The RAND finding about transportation and the one about high schoolers working on the off day bear on the counterargument you planned.
- No research separates marching band from "extracurriculars" in general. You'll need to decide how much weight general findings can carry for band.
