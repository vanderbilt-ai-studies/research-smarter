## Sources

**Research organization reports**

- **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Can four equal five? Assessing the four-day school week* (RB-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://doi.org/10.7249/RBA373-1
  A 4-page summary of a six-state study that surveyed students and parents. It reports that "on the fifth day, most districts offered practices for sports or other competitions; some districts held clubs or student activities." It also says 80% of high schoolers spent the day off mostly at home.
- **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five?   The full 202-page report behind the brief. Its landing page says high schoolers on four-day schedules reported about 3.5 more hours of free time per week. I read only the landing page, not the full PDF, so I can't say what it says about activities in detail.

**Peer-reviewed article**

- **Peer-reviewed article** (*Education Finance and Policy*). Thompson, P. N., Gunter, K. B., Schuna, J. M., Jr., & Tomayko, E. J. (2021). Are all four-day school weeks created equal? A national assessment of four-day school week policy adoption and implementation. *Education Finance and Policy, 16*(4), 558–583.
  I couldn't open the publisher's page because access was blocked. I confirmed the journal and details through the OpenAlex database record, which includes the abstract, and that's all I read. **Policy brief**

- **Policy organization brief.** National Conference of State Legislatures. (2023, June 28). *Four-day school week overview*. https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview
  It says a longer weekend gives students more time for extracurriculars, but it cites no study for that claim. It also warns that "lack of transportation could limit access" to anything held on the fifth day.

## Questions these results raise

- If the other four days are about an hour longer, rehearsal time might move rather than disappear. Your position depends on whether that happens.
- RAND found the fifth day was often used for sports practices. Whether band gets the same treatment is still open.
- The NCSL note about transportation connects to your counterargument about students who have no ride on the fifth day.
