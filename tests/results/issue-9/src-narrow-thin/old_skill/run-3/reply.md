## Sources

**Research reports and peer-reviewed studies**

- Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Can four equal five? Assessing the four-day school week* (Research Brief RB-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RBA373-1.html
  This 4-page summary covers 36 four-day districts in Idaho, New Mexico, and Oklahoma. On the fifth day, "most districts offered practices for sports or other competitions," and "some districts held clubs or student activities." Achievement in four-day districts grew more slowly than in five-day districts.
- Kilburn, M. R., et al. (2021). *Does four equal five? Implementation and outcomes of the four-day school week* (Research Report RR-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA373-1.html
  This is the full 202-page report behind the brief. Its summary page says high schoolers on four-day weeks reported about 3.5 more hours of free time per week. I couldn't read the PDF itself, so I haven't seen what it says about activities in detail.
- Thompson, P. N., Tomayko, E. J., Gunter, K. B., Schuna, J., Jr., & McClelland, M. (2023). Impacts of the four-day school week on early elementary achievement. *Early Childhood Research Quarterly, 63*, 264–277. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9979633 (DOI listed on that page: 10.1016/j.ecresq.2022.12.009)
  This **peer-reviewed** study followed Oregon kindergartners into 3rd grade and found little average difference in test scores. Higher-achieving students were hurt most, possibly because they miss "enrichment activities." It's about young children, not high school.
- Morton, E., Thompson, P. N., & Kuhfeld, M. (2024). A multi-state, student-level analysis of the effects of the four-day school week on student achievement and growth. *Economics of Education Review.* I found this **peer-reviewed** study in search results but couldn't open it (blocked), so check it before relying on it. Secondhand summaries say achievement fell more in town and suburban four-day districts than in rural ones.
- Holmes, W. E. (2025). *Improving the work-life balance of the high school marching band director: A case study* [Doctoral dissertation, Liberty University]. https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/doctoral/6700
  This is a qualitative case study of one marching band that went from rehearsing four days a week to two. According to the abstract, competition scores didn't change significantly. It's about how often the band rehearsed, not about a four-day school calendar. I read only the abstract.

**Policy overviews**

- National Conference of State Legislatures. (2023, June 28). *Four-day school week overview.* https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview
  It says the longer weekend gives students "more time to rest and engage in extracurricular activities." It also warns that "if optional activities were offered by schools on the fifth day, lack of transportation could limit access."

**News and practitioner writing**

- Grundmeier, N. (2025, August 26). Pass or fail? Midwest families and districts are learning from the 4-day school week. *KCUR.* https://www.kcur.org/2025-08-26/pass-or-fail-midwest-families-and-districts-are-learning-from-the-4-day-school-week

- Simonton, S. (2019, December 11). Is four-day school week education mistake or big opportunity for expanded learning? *Youth Today.* https://youthtoday.org/2019/12/is-four-day-school-week-education-mistake-or-big-opportunity-for-expanded-learning/
