## Sources

**Studies and reports on four-day weeks in general**

1. Thompson, P. N., Gunter, K., Schuna, J. M., Jr., & Tomayko, E. J. (2021). Are all four-day school weeks created equal? A national assessment of four-day school week policy adoption and implementation. *Education Finance and Policy, 16*(4), 558–583. https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00316
   Built a dataset of 1,600+ four-day schools in 24 states. Found that adoption is mostly rural and money-driven, and that four-day schools average about 148 days a year against 180 for five-day schools, with longer days. *I confirmed the details on [its RePEc listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:tpr:edfpol:v:16:y:2021:i:4:p:558-583). The DOI does lead to the publisher's page, but that page wouldn't let me in (paywall), so I've read the abstract only.*
2. Turner, J., Finch, K., & Uribe-Zarain, X. (2019). Three Midwest rural school districts' first year transition to the four day school week: Parents' perspectives. *The Rural Educator, 40*(1), 1–15. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1225164
   Survey of 584 parents in three rural Missouri districts in their first four-day year. Support was strong overall but lower among families with only elementary-age children or with special-education students. ERIC lists it as peer reviewed. The abstract doesn't mention extracurriculars.
3. Ongaga, K. O. (2026). Beyond achievement: Principals' perceptions of the four-day school week in rural Missouri. *International Journal of Educational Methodology, 12*(3), 241–257. https://www.ijem.com/beyond-achievement-principals-perceptions-of-the-four-day-school-week-in-rural-missouri
   Compared surveys of 94 four-day principals with 70 five-day principals. Four-day principals reported more planning time and high satisfaction, and the author says these are associations, not causes. The part I read doesn't mention band or arts. *The page lists DOI 10.12973/ijem.12.3.241, but I opened only the article page, not the DOI link.*
4. Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C., Mariano, L., Doss, C., Troxel, W., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five? Implementation and outcomes of the four-day school week*. RAND Corporation / NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/does-four-equal-five-implementation-and-outcomes-of-the-four-day-school-week/
   Study of districts in Idaho, New Mexico, and Oklahoma. Found small cost savings, high family satisfaction, and slower ELA and math growth over time. I could open the listing page but couldn't read the full report text, so the time-use findings below come from Education Week's coverage of it.
5. Morton, E., Thompson, P., & Kuhfeld, M. (2023). *A multi-state, student-level analysis of the effects of the four-day school week on student achievement and growth* (EdWorkingPaper No. 22-630). Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai22-630
   Found small negative effects on reading achievement and on math and reading growth, larger in non-rural schools. This is the working-paper version. Search results say a version was published in *Economics of Education Review* (2024), but I didn't open that journal page, so check it before citing it as peer reviewed.

**Marching band rehearsal research**

6. Holmes, W. E. (2025). *Improving the work-life balance of the high school marching band director: A case study* [Doctoral dissertation, Liberty University]. Scholars Crossing. https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/doctoral/6700
   Compared one band's competition scores in a year with four after-school rehearsals a week against a year with two. Reports no significant drop in scores, plus more tutoring time and better director work-life balance. It's about rehearsal frequency, not four-day school weeks. I read only the abstract.
7. Smith, S. (2015, August). The ideal marching schedule. *The Instrumentalist*. https://theinstrumentalist.com/august-2015/the-ideal-marching-schedule/
   A band director's opinion piece arguing that marching band belongs after school so class time can go to concert band. He reports his program grew from 23 to about 70 students. It doesn't mention four-day weeks.

**News and policy summaries**

8. Sawchuk, S. (2021, October 7). 4-day school weeks: New research examines the benefits and drawbacks. *Education Week*. https://edweek.org/leadership/4-day-school-weeks-new-research-examines-the-benefits-and-drawbacks/2021/10
   Covers the RAND study. Reports that four-day students "spent significantly more time on school sports," and that secondary students spent more time "at jobs, at school activities, and on hobbies." About 80% of high schoolers spent the off day at home.
9. National Conference of State Legislatures. (2023, June 28). *Four-day school week overview*. https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview
   Policy brief. Notes that some schools offer optional activities on the fifth day and that "lack of transportation could limit access" to them.
10. Peetz Stephens, C. (2026, April 9). The 4-day school week: What research shows about the alternative schedule. *Education Week*. https://www.edweek.org/leadership/the-4-day-school-week-what-research-shows-about-the-alternative-schedule/2026/04
    Explainer reporting more than 2,100 schools in 26 states on four-day weeks as of 2024–25. Covers academic effects, cost savings, and how districts use the off day. Band and extracurriculars aren't discussed.
11. Ordway, D.-M. (2025, August 1; updated 2025, September 16). Four-day school week: Research shows the impact of a condensed schedule varies by school location and student grade level. *The Journalist's Resource*. https://journalistsresource.org/education/four-day-school-week-research/
    Overview of the research, which it calls limited. Mentions lost access to PE and possibly to enrichment activities. Doesn't mention music.

## Questions these results raise

- Most marching bands rehearse after school. Does a four-day week actually remove a rehearsal, or does it remove a school day? Those aren't necessarily the same thing.
- The Holmes case study found that halving rehearsals didn't lower scores. How does that sit with the "lose a day" mechanism in your position?
- The RAND time-use data shows more time on school activities and on jobs. Your counterargument response mentions both.
