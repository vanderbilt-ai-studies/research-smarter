**Scholarly and reference sources**

- "Philadelphia, Pennsylvania and the 1918–1919 Influenza Epidemic." *Influenza Encyclopedia.* University of Michigan Center for the History of Medicine and Michigan Publishing. https://www.influenzaarchive.org/f/flu/cities/city-philadelphia.html
  An essay on the city's epidemic. It quotes Health Director Wilmer Krusen's public statements and reports that the *Inquirer*'s editorial board opposed the closure orders as creating panic, while the *Evening Bulletin* supported them. The same archive has digitized newspaper articles you can search.
- Wirth, Thomas. "Influenza ('Spanish Flu' Pandemic, 1918–19)." *Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia.* Rutgers-Camden, 2011. https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/archive/influenza-spanish-flu-pandemic-1918-19/
  Says the major papers reported on September 21 that the cause had been identified. The *Inquirer* claimed this "armed the medical profession with absolute knowledge." Officials then presented the disease as controllable.
- Wirth, Thomas. "Urban Neglect: The Environment, Public Health, and Influenza in Philadelphia, 1915–1919." *Pennsylvania History* 73, no. 3 (2006): 316–42. I found it in search results but couldn't open it, because Project MUSE blocked access. Check it through your library before relying on it.

## Questions the results raise

- The papers may not have moved as a group. The *Inquirer* and the *Bulletin* reportedly took opposite sides on the closures.
- The *Inquirer*'s October 5 editorial came after deaths were already mounting. That bears on what "then panicked" means and when it happened.
- Some of the early reassurance came from officials quoted in the papers. That raises the question of whose voice the downplaying really was.
