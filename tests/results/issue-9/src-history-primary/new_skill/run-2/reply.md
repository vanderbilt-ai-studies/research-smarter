**Secondary sources**

- **Peer-reviewed article (*Public Health Reports*).** Gamble, Vanessa Northington. "'There Wasn't a Lot of Comforts in Those Days': African Americans, Public Health, and the 1918 Influenza Epidemic." *Public Health Reports* 125, suppl. 3 (2010): 114–22. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2862340
  How Black communities experienced the epidemic and how their institutions responded, drawing on Black newspapers. It says the *Philadelphia Tribune*'s coverage was "muted" and didn't reflect how big the crisis was. The paper did run four front-page flu stories on October 19, 1918.

- **Peer-reviewed article (*PNAS*).** Hatchett, Richard J., Carter E. Mecher, and Marc Lipsitch. "Public Health Interventions and Epidemic Intensity during the 1918 Influenza Pandemic." *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 104, no. 18 (2007): 7582–87. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1849867
  Compares public health measures in 17 U.S. cities and uses Philadelphia and St. Louis as the main contrast. It dates Philadelphia's first civilian cases to September 17, the parade to September 28, and the closure orders to October 3. It's about public health measures, not press coverage. It's useful for building your timeline.

- **Scholarly reference entry (Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia, Rutgers–Camden; essay © University of Pennsylvania Press).** Wirth, Thomas. "Influenza ('Spanish Flu' Pandemic, 1918–19)." *Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia*, 2011. https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/archive/influenza-spanish-flu-pandemic-1918-19/
  An overview of the epidemic in the city. It quotes the September 21 *Inquirer* saying a researcher's findings had "armed the medical profession with absolute knowledge" against the disease. It also cites a letter to the *Public Ledger* and *Evening Bulletin* arrest reports from October.

- **University research project (University of Michigan Center for the History of Medicine).** "Philadelphia, Pennsylvania." *The American Influenza Epidemic of 1918–1919: A Digital Encyclopedia*. University of Michigan Center for the History of Medicine and Michigan Publishing. https://www.influenzaarchive.org/f/flu/cities/city-philadelphia.html
  A detailed account of the city's epidemic, built heavily on *Inquirer* and *Evening Bulletin* articles. It describes the *Inquirer* arguing that the closure orders went too far while the *Bulletin* called them necessary. It also says the health director, Wilmer Krusen, lacked the authority to cancel the parade; that power was the mayor's.

## What I found and didn't find

There's plenty on the epidemic in Philadelphia and the parade, and plenty of popular articles (History.com, the *New Republic*, PhillyVoice) saying the city's papers played it down. I didn't find a single peer-reviewed study that systematically analyzes what Philadelphia newspapers printed in 1918. Most published claims about editors' motives come from popular histories, especially John M. Barry's *The Great Influenza*, rather than from journal research. That gap is a real opening for your paper, and it means your own reading of the primary sources will carry a lot of weight.
