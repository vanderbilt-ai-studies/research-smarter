5. **Journal article** (*Pennsylvania History*, Penn State University Press. I saw it described as peer reviewed but didn't confirm that on the journal's own page). Stetler, Christina M. "The 1918 Spanish Influenza: Three Months of Horror in Philadelphia." *Pennsylvania History: A Journal of Mid-Atlantic Studies* 84, no. 4 (2017): 462–87. https://doi.org/10.5325/pennhistory.84.4.0462. [Free PDF](https://pa-history.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/PAH-84.4_04_Stetler.pdf)
   A narrative of the epidemic in Philadelphia from September 1918 to March 1919, built largely on *Evening Bulletin* and *Inquirer* articles. ## What I found and didn't find

- **Plenty:** the course of the epidemic in Philadelphia, and places to find the 1918 papers themselves.
- **Thin:** scholarship centered on *how Philadelphia's press covered it*. I found no peer-reviewed article on exactly that. The press claims you'll see most often online (papers refusing to print doctors' warnings before the parade, threats under the Sedition Act) trace back mostly to John Barry's *The Great Influenza*, a popular history, quoted in magazines. I didn't find a scholarly source that confirms them.
- **Not found:** anything on the editors' own reasons, such as letters or memoirs.

## Issues the results raise

- Stetler shows that some of the early reassurance in the papers was quoted from the city's own health officials and doctors. That bears on your counterargument about who knew what. Your sources will need to separate what the papers chose to say from what they were told.
- Tomes's picture of heavy flu coverage alongside the war doesn't easily fit "downplayed," and it doesn't easily fit "panicked" either. It may depend on which weeks and which papers you look at.
