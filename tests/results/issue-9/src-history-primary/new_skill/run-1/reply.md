**Secondary sources**

1. **Scholarly journal article.** Stetler, Christina M. "The 1918 Spanish Influenza: Three Months of Horror in Philadelphia." *Pennsylvania History: A Journal of Mid-Atlantic Studies* 84, no. 4 (2017): 463–87. [PDF](https://pa-history.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/PAH-84.4_04_Stetler.pdf)

I couldn't confirm peer review on the journal's own site (the publisher's page wouldn't open), so check that.

2. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Medical History*, which says on its Cambridge page that it is refereed). Honigsbaum, Mark. "Regulating the 1918–19 Pandemic: Flu, Stoicism and the Northcliffe Press." *Medical History* 57, no. 2 (2013): 165–85. https://doi.org/10.1017/mdh.2012.101
   This article is about the **British** press, not Philadelphia. It looks at how the Northcliffe papers (*Daily Mail*, *The Times*) promoted wartime stoicism about the flu, and how fear of the disease undercut that message. I read only part of the full text.

3. **Scholarly book** (Oxford University Press). Bristow, Nancy K. *American Pandemic: The Lost Worlds of the 1918 Influenza Epidemic*. New York: Oxford University Press, 2012. [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9780199811342)
   A social history of how ordinary Americans lived through the pandemic and how it was later forgotten. Stetler relies on it for her claims about newspaper tone. I confirmed the book exists but haven't read it.

4. **Scholarly book** (Cambridge University Press). Crosby, Alfred W. *America's Forgotten Pandemic: The Influenza of 1918*. 2nd ed. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2003. [Chapter 1 page](https://cambridge.org/core/books/abs/americas-forgotten-pandemic/great-shadow/75BC6676344733F34BA20ABC3E2EB34E)

5. **Scholarly reference essay.** Wirth, Thomas. "Influenza ('Spanish Flu' Pandemic, 1918–19)." *Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia*. 2011. [Link](https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/archive/influenza-spanish-flu-pandemic-1918-19/)

6. **University digital reference essay.** "Philadelphia, Pennsylvania and the 1918–1919 Influenza Epidemic." *The American Influenza Epidemic of 1918–1919: A Digital Encyclopedia*. ## What I found and didn't find

- **Plenty:** Philadelphia newspaper material from September and October 1918. There are dated headlines, a free digitized daily (the *Ledger*) and clipping scrapbooks.
- **Thin:** scholarship that analyzes the Philadelphia papers as its main subject. I found no journal article whose main focus is how Philadelphia papers covered the epidemic. Stetler and the two encyclopedia essays use newspapers as evidence about the epidemic rather than studying them. The closest press study is Honigsbaum's, and it's about Britain.
- **Couldn't find:** anything documenting a federal censorship order aimed at flu coverage in Philadelphia. Popular articles say the Sedition Act made editors cautious, but I found no scholarly source showing how that affected Philadelphia papers specifically.
- **Worth noticing:** the dated headlines in source 6 include early-October *Inquirer* pieces that criticize the closing orders for feeding fear. The *Inquirer* and *Evening Bulletin* also didn't always take the same line.
