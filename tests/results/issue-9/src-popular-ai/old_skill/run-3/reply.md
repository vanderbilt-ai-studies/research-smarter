## Sources

**Peer-reviewed studies (experiments)**

1. Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122*(26), e2422633122.
   A field experiment with nearly 1,000 high school math students in Turkey. Students got either a plain ChatGPT-style tutor or a tutor built with learning safeguards. Both groups did better on practice problems while they had the AI. Once it was taken away, the plain-ChatGPT group did worse on the exam than students who never had AI. The safeguarded tutor mostly avoided that drop. *No link: the publisher's site blocked me. I confirmed the article and its details in Crossref, the DOI registry, which also lists a correction notice from August 2025. Look up the title through your library.*

2. Kestin, G., Miller, K., Klales, A., Milbourne, T., & Ponti, G. (2025). AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: An RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting. *Scientific Reports, 15*, Article 17458. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/
   A randomized trial with 194 Harvard students in an intro physics course. Students learned more with a custom-built AI tutor than in an active-learning class, in less time (a median of 49 minutes versus 60), and said they felt more engaged.

3. Fan, Y., Tang, L., Le, H., Shen, K., Tan, S., Zhao, Y., Shen, Y., Li, X., & Gašević, D. (2025). Beware of metacognitive laziness: Effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning motivation, processes, and performance. *British Journal of Educational Technology, 56*(2), 489–530.
   A lab experiment with 117 university students doing a writing task. Each student got one of four kinds of support: ChatGPT, a human expert, writing-analytics tools, or nothing extra. The ChatGPT group improved their essays the most, but they didn't gain more knowledge or transfer it better than the other groups. *No link: the publisher blocked me. I worked from the Crossref record's abstract.*

**Peer-reviewed reviews and surveys**

4. Deng, R., Jiang, M., Yu, X., Lu, Y., & Liu, S. (2025). Does ChatGPT enhance student learning? A systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental studies. *Computers & Education, 227*, Article 105224.
   A meta-analysis of 69 experimental studies from 2022–2024. According to search-result summaries, it found gains in performance and higher-order thinking and no change in self-efficacy. *I confirmed the citation in Crossref but couldn't open the article, so this summary is secondhand. No link.*

5. Zhai, C., Wibowo, S., & Li, L. D. (2024). The effects of over-reliance on AI dialogue systems on students' cognitive abilities: A systematic review. *Smart Learning Environments, 11*, Article 28.
   A review of 14 articles. It concludes that leaning heavily on AI chatbots, and preferring quick answers to effortful reasoning, can weaken critical and analytical thinking. *Publisher page blocked me. I worked from the Crossref abstract. No link.*

6. Gerlich, M. (2025). AI tools in society: Impacts on cognitive offloading and the future of critical thinking. *Societies, 15*(1), Article 6.
   A survey of 666 people plus interviews. More frequent AI use went with lower critical-thinking scores, partly explained by "cognitive offloading," and younger participants relied on AI most. The author says this shows correlation, not cause. *I couldn't open the article. Crossref lists a correction, but I couldn't see what it changes. Summary is from search results. No link.*

**Preprints (not yet peer-reviewed)**

7. Lehmann, M., Cornelius, P. B., & Sting, F. J. (2025). *AI meets the classroom: When do large language models harm learning?* (arXiv:2409.09047). arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047
   Two pre-registered lab experiments found no overall effect of LLMs on learning. Using the AI to *substitute* for learning activities let students cover more topics but with less understanding of each. Using it to *complement* their own work, such as asking for explanations, improved understanding. The AI also widened the gap between students with low and high prior knowledge.

8. Kosmyna, N., Hauptmann, E., Yuan, Y. T., Situ, J., Liao, X.-H., Beresnitzky, A. V., Braunstein, I., & Maes, P. (2025). *Your brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of cognitive debt when using an AI assistant for essay writing task* (arXiv:2506.08872). arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872
   54 participants wrote essays with ChatGPT, with a search engine, or with no tools, while wearing EEG headsets. The ChatGPT group showed the weakest brain connectivity and the lowest sense of owning their essays. Only 18 people completed the final session.

## What I found and didn't find

- **Thin on high schoolers.** Only Bastani studied high school students directly. Most of the experiments used university students or adults. The Pew surveys cover teens but measure use, not learning.
- **Thin on long-term effects.** Nearly everything measures learning over days or weeks.
- **Few studies separate kinds of use.** Lehmann (substitute vs. complement) and Bastani (plain vs. safeguarded tool) come closest. I didn't find a study that compares students who check their work with students who copy answers in those exact terms.
- **A retracted source to avoid:** Wang & Fan (2025), a meta-analysis in *Humanities & Social Sciences Communications*, came up often in my searches with big positive effect sizes, but Crossref shows it has been retracted. I left it off the list. If you see it quoted elsewhere, treat that as a warning sign about the source quoting it.

## Questions these results raise

- Bastani's safeguards were built into the *tool*, while Lehmann's split is about student *behavior*. Your position is about what students choose to do, so how those two fit together is worth thinking through.
- Several studies found students did better *while using* the AI but not on tests afterward. That gap between performance and learning could matter for how you define "help."
