## Sources

**Peer-reviewed studies**

1. **Peer-reviewed article** (*PNAS*; I confirmed the venue on the PubMed Central page).
   Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122*(26), e2422633122. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/
   This was a field experiment with nearly 1,000 high school math students (grades 9–11) in Turkey. Some students got a plain ChatGPT-style tool, some got a tutor version that gave hints instead of answers, and some got no AI. Both AI groups scored higher on practice problems. On a later exam without AI, the plain-tool group scored 17% lower than the no-AI group, while the hint-based group showed almost no drop. The page notes that a correction was published, which I didn't open.

3. **Peer-reviewed article** (*British Journal of Educational Technology*; venue confirmed through Crossref and OpenAlex).
   Fan, Y., Tang, L., Le, H., Shen, K., Tan, S., Zhao, Y., Shen, Y., Li, X., & Gašević, D. (2025). Beware of metacognitive laziness: Effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning motivation, processes, and performance. *British Journal of Educational Technology, 56*(2), 489–530.
   In this randomized experiment, 117 university students did a writing task with ChatGPT, a human expert, writing-analytics tools, or no help. 4. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Computers in Human Behavior*; venue confirmed through Crossref and OpenAlex).
   Stadler, M., Bannert, M., & Sailer, M. (2024). Cognitive ease at a cost: LLMs reduce mental effort but compromise depth in student scientific inquiry. *Computers in Human Behavior, 160*, 108386.
   91 university students researched a science question using either ChatGPT-3.5 or Google. The ChatGPT group found the task less mentally demanding but wrote recommendations with weaker reasoning. *I read the abstract through a database; the publisher page wouldn't open.*

5. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Scientific Reports*; venue confirmed on the PubMed Central page).
   Kestin, G., Miller, K., Klales, A., Milbourne, T., & Ponti, G. (2025). AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: An RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting. *Scientific Reports, 15*, 17458. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/
   This randomized trial with Harvard intro physics students compared a custom AI tutor, built on the same teaching principles as the class, with in-class active learning. Students who used the AI tutor learned more in less time.

**Preprint**

7. **Preprint, not yet peer reviewed.**
   Lehmann, M., Cornelius, P. B., & Sting, F. J. (2025). *AI meets the classroom: When do large language models harm learning?* (arXiv:2409.09047v2) [Preprint]. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047

## What I found and didn't find

- **Peer-reviewed studies on high schoolers are thin.** Bastani et al. is the only one I found that studies high schoolers directly, and Zhu et al. studies middle schoolers. Most experiments use college students.
- **No study I found compares "checking work" with "getting answers" for high school homework directly.** The closest are Bastani (which compared answer-giving and hint-giving tools) and Lehmann (which compared substitute use with complementary use, but is a preprint).
- **There are almost no long-term studies.** Most studies run for a single task or one semester.
- **The Pew surveys measure use and opinions, not learning.**
- **A warning:** a widely shared 2025 meta-analysis by Wang & Fan in *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* was **retracted in April 2026**. You'll probably run into it in searches, and blogs still cite it. I left it out.

## Questions these results raise

- In Bastani, the *tool's design* decided whether students got answers. In your position, the *student's choice* decides.
- Kestin's AI tutor helped college students, but it was purpose-built to teach, not a general chatbot like the one a teen would use at home.
