## Sources

**Research on four-day weeks that mentions activities or the off day**

1. **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five? Implementation and outcomes of the four-day school week* (Report No. RR-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://doi.org/10.7249/RR-A373-1
   This is a large study of districts in several states using surveys, interviews and test scores. It found that students on four-day and five-day weeks spent similar time each week on school sports and school activities (p. 39). Most districts held sports practices on the off day, but "no districts offered transportation to fifth-day activities" (p. 43). In one district, parents said their children "had band practice on the fifth day" (p. 47). It isn't peer reviewed, but it's the most detailed study of what happens on the off day. Band comes up in only one line.

2. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Education Finance and Policy*). Thompson, P. N., Gunter, K. B., Schuna, J. M., & Tomayko, E. J. (2021). Are all four-day school weeks created equal? A national assessment of four-day school week policy adoption and implementation. *Education Finance and Policy, 16*(4), 558–583. https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00316
   The authors built a national dataset of four-day calendars. The off day is often Friday and the remaining days run nearly an hour longer than average. Four-day schedules average 148 school days a year, compared with the national average of 180. I could only read the abstract because the article is paywalled.

3. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Education Economics*). Thompson, P. N., Tomayko, E. J., Gunter, K. B., & Schuna, J. M. (2022). Impacts of the four-day school week on high school achievement and educational engagement. *Education Economics, 30*(5), 527–539. https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2021.2006610
   In Oregon, students who started a four-day week in high school had lower 11th-grade math scores, more chronic absence and lower on-time graduation. It doesn't mention band. I read only the abstract because it's paywalled. OpenAlex lists the year as 2021, probably the online date, while volume 30 is the print issue, so check which year your library shows.

4. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Journal of School Health*). Israel, W., Mulitauopele, C., Ma, M., Levinson, A. H., Cikara, L., & Brooks-Russell, A. (2020). Adolescent health behaviors in schools with 4- versus 5-day school weeks. *Journal of School Health, 90*(10), 794–801. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.12941
   This study used a Colorado survey of high school students. A four-day week was linked to "improved student engagement" and more physical activity, but also to more bullying and less sleep. The RAND report says this study found four-day students were *more* likely to take part in extracurricular activities. I read only the PubMed abstract, which doesn't say that directly, so check the full text before you cite that claim.

5. **Research organization report.** HEDCO Institute for Evidence-Based Educational Practice. (2025). *Does a four-day school week benefit students?* University of Oregon. https://hedcoinstitute.uoregon.edu/reports/four-day-school-week
   This systematic review of 11 studies found "little evidence of positive outcomes" for students. The university's news release about it says keeping youth-development activities going on the off day matters. The report page shows no date, so I took 2025 from that news release, which is dated June 30, 2025. The review doesn't measure extracurriculars.

**Band-specific sources**

6. **Dissertation, not peer reviewed.** Holmes, W. E. (2025). *Improving the work-life balance of the high school marching band director: A case study* [Doctoral dissertation, Liberty University]. Scholars Crossing. https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/doctoral/6700
   This is a case study of one band. It compared a year with four after-school rehearsals a week to a year with two, and found "no significant change in competitive marching band scores." It's about after-school rehearsals, not the school calendar, and it covers only one band. I read only the abstract.

7. **Primary source (state rules body).** University Interscholastic League. (n.d.). *Eight hour marching band rule FAQ*. https://www.uiltexas.org/music/marching-band/eight-hour-marching-band-rule-qa
   Texas caps marching band rehearsal outside the school day at 8 hours a week. Weeks with fewer than five school days get an extra hour for each non-school day, within limits. It's a rule, not evidence of effects. It shows that in at least one state, rehearsal is capped by hours rather than days. The page's own examples are a bit inconsistent, so check with UIL or a Texas director.

**Policy overview and reporting**

8. **Research organization report.** National Conference of State Legislatures. (2023, June 28). *Four-day school week overview*. https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview
   This is an overview for legislators. It notes that "if optional activities were offered by schools on the fifth day, lack of transportation could limit access." It also lists "more time to … engage in extracurricular activities" as an argument supporters make. That's a claim, not a finding.

9. **News article.** Grundmeier, N. (2025, August 26). Pass or fail? Midwest families and districts are learning from the 4-day school week. *Iowa Public Radio*. https://www.iowapublicradio.org/ipr-news/2025-08-26/pass-or-fail-midwest-families-and-districts-are-learning-from-the-4-day-school-week
   This is reporting on Iowa and Missouri districts. A researcher quoted in it says a four-day week means "one fewer day when students have access to P.E., art and music." Iowa law keeps total instructional hours the same however many days there are. My tool couldn't load the end of the page. A summary it gave me said the article mentions bands practicing on the off day, but I couldn't confirm that wording. Use news like this for current context, not as evidence of effects.

## Questions the results raise

- Your position counts *days*, but several sources measure *hours*. The school day is longer, Iowa keeps total hours fixed, and Texas caps rehearsal by the week. It's not yet clear which measure matters for a marching band.
- RAND shows the off day being used for practices. The gaps it names are transportation and supervision, which link to your counterargument about rides and jobs.
- No one seems to have counted band enrollment before and after a switch. That gap could be part of your argument.
