**Research organization reports**

3. **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five?    This is a large study of districts in Idaho, New Mexico, and Oklahoma. It found that students in grades 7–12 on four-day schedules reported about 3.5 more hours of free time per week. Reports like this are reviewed internally, not by a journal.

4. **Research organization report (brief).** Kilburn, M. R., et al. (2021). *Can four equal five? Assessing the four-day school week* (RB-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_briefs/RBA300/RBA373-1/RAND_RBA373-1.pdf
   This is the short version of the report above. It says that on the fifth day "most districts offered practices for sports or other competitions; some districts held clubs or student activities," and that most students spent that day at home. It's useful as a quick read, but cite the full report.

5. **Research organization report (systematic review).** Day, Golfen, Grant, & Trevino. (2025). *Does a four-day school week benefit students?* HEDCO Institute, University of Oregon. https://hedcoinstitute.uoregon.edu/does-four-day-school-week-benefit-students-0
   The copy I opened didn't show the authors' first initials. The review covers 11 studies with causal evidence. In rural high schools the results are mixed, including higher graduation rates and more chronic absence. It says districts should make sure "co-curricular activities, or sports" are available on the fifth day. It doesn't look at band.
