### Sources

**Peer-reviewed articles** (these three meet your 2-peer-reviewed requirement; I confirmed each journal through Crossref or Europe PMC)

1. **Peer-reviewed article.** Thompson, P. N., Gunter, K., Schuna, J. M., Jr., & Tomayko, E. J. (2021). Are all four-day school weeks created equal? A national assessment of four-day school week policy adoption and implementation. *Education Finance and Policy, 16*(4), 558–583. https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00316
   A national dataset of four-day schedules. The day off is often Friday, and the other four days are on average nearly an hour longer than the national five-day average. These schools average 148 days a year, compared with 180. *I could only read the abstract; the publisher's page blocked me.*

2. **Peer-reviewed article.** Tomayko, E. J., Thompson, P. N., Smith, M. C., Gunter, K. B., & Schuna, J. M. (2021). Impact of reduced school exposure on adolescent health behaviors and food security: Evidence from 4-day school weeks. *Journal of School Health.* https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.13095
   Uses Oregon survey data from about 100,000 8th and 11th graders. Students at four-day schools reported fewer days of physical education than students at five-day schools, along with other health differences. *Abstract only.*

3. **Peer-reviewed article.** Thompson, P. N., Tomayko, E. J., Gunter, K. B., & Schuna, J. (2022). Impacts of the four-day school week on high school achievement and educational engagement. *Education Economics, 30*(5). https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2021.2006610
   Oregon students who started a four-day week in high school had slightly lower 11th-grade math scores, more chronic absence, and lower on-time graduation. It doesn't measure activities. *Abstract only.*

**Research organization reports**

4. **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five? Implementation and outcomes of the four-day school week* (RR-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://doi.org/10.7249/RR-A373-1
   Surveys of families in 36 districts in three states. Students in grades 7–12 on four-day schedules reported about 3.5 more hours of free time a week. *I could read the summary page but not the full PDF.* Why it's here: the largest study of how four-day students actually spend their time.

5. **Research organization brief.** National Conference of State Legislatures. (2023, June 28). *Brief four-day school week overview.* https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview
   Lists "more time to… engage in extracurricular activities" as a possible benefit. It also warns that "lack of transportation could limit access" to optional activities on the day off. Why it's here: a nonpartisan policy summary. It reports claims and concerns but no new data.

6. **Research organization report.** HEDCO Institute for Evidence-Based Educational Practice. (n.d.). *Four-day school week technical report: Tables.* University of Oregon. https://hedco-institute.github.io/4DSW_Scoping_Review/technical_report/tables.html
   A scoping review that catalogued research on four-day weeks. Extracurriculars, clubs, or sports on the fifth day show up in 12 documents (9%), and 59% of documents don't report what happens that day at all. Why it's here: it shows how little the research tracks activities.

**Policy journal and news**

7. **Policy journal article, not peer reviewed.** Thompson, P. N. (2021). The shrinking school week: Effects of a four-day schedule on student achievement. *Education Next, 21*(3), 60–67. https://www.educationnext.org/shrinking-school-week-effects-four-day-schedule-student-achievement/
   A researcher's overview. Of 552 districts that reported, about half were fully closed on the day off and about 30% offered remedial or enrichment programs. He calls for research on what students do with that day. Why it's here: an expert summary, not original data.

8. **News article.** Sawchuk, S. (2021, October 7). 4-day school weeks: New research examines the benefits and drawbacks. *Education Week.* https://www.edweek.org/leadership/4-day-school-weeks-new-research-examines-the-benefits-and-drawbacks/2021/10
   Reports the RAND findings: 80% of high schoolers spent the day off mostly at home, and four-day students spent significantly more time on school sports. Older students also spent more time at jobs and school activities. Why it's here: it's the only readable account of those RAND details. Check them against the full RAND report.

9. **News article.** CBS Colorado. (2019, April 29). Teacher sees success in orchestra academy under new 4-day schedule. *CBS News Colorado.* https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/orchestra-academy-27j-school-district
   A music teacher in Colorado's 27J district started a fee-based orchestra program on the Monday off day. It grew from 27 to 40 students, mostly elementary. Why it's here: the only real-world music example I found. It's one program and reported by news, so it's an example, not evidence of a trend.

**Dissertation**

10. **Dissertation, not peer reviewed.** Holmes, W. E. (2025). *Improving the work-life balance of the high school marching band director: A case study* [Doctoral dissertation, Liberty University]. https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/doctoral/6700

Why it's here: the only marching-band rehearsal study I found. It's one band, and it isn't about four-day school weeks. *Abstract only.*

### Questions the results raise

- Four-day schedules usually make the other days longer. That could change after-school rehearsal time as well as the school day itself.
- The day off is often Friday, which is football game night for most marching bands.
- Studies say students spend more time on school sports, but not whether band counts as one of those "school activities."
- The real gap may be in the research itself, not only in band programs.
