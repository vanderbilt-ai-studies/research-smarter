2. **Peer-reviewed article (cross-sectional).** Goodyear, V. A., Randhawa, A., Adab, P., Al-Janabi, H., Fenton, S., Jones, K., Michail, M., Morrison, B., Patterson, P., Quinlan, J., Sitch, A., Twardochleb, R., Wade, M., & Pallan, M. (2025). 4. **Peer-reviewed article (longitudinal survey).** Vereecke, J., Vanherle, R., Nassen, L.-M., Boen, H., Eggermont, S., Beullens, K., Vandenbosch, L., & Schreurs, L. (2026). More than disconnecting?    It followed 960 Belgian secondary students across eight survey waves around a total ban. Social loneliness rose modestly, friend support stayed stable, and cyberbullying perpetration fell slightly. The study had no comparison group of schools without a ban.
5. **Peer-reviewed article (qualitative interviews).** Bisom-Rapp, E., Otmar, C. D., Potes, C., Nayak, S., Engle, K., Ganson, K. T., & Nagata, J. M. (2026). 6. **Peer-reviewed article (natural experiment).** Beneito, P., & Vicente-Chirivella, Ó. (2022). Banning mobile phones in schools: Evidence from regional-level policies in Spain. *Applied Economic Analysis, 30*(90), 153–175. https://doi.org/10.1108/AEA-05-2021-0112

**Working papers (not peer reviewed)**

9. **Working paper, not peer reviewed.** Allcott, H., Baron, E. J., Dee, T., Duckworth, A. L., Gentzkow, M., & Jacob, B. ## Coverage report

- **What I searched:** both sub-questions, in about 10 web searches with different wordings (bans and interaction, lunch and break-time socializing, bullying, cyberbullying, after-school displacement). I also ran Crossref and PubMed searches and followed citations from the strongest studies.
- **Where evidence is plentiful:** phone use during school and wellbeing. Bullying in general has a moderate amount, including two natural-experiment studies.
- **Where evidence is thin:**
  - Face-to-face interaction is measured almost only through what students report in surveys and interviews. I found no study where researchers observed it directly.
  - I found **no study that directly measures whether conflict moves to after school.** Several studies mention it only as background or as a question for future research.
  - Studies of US high schools specifically are few.

## Questions these results raise

- Several studies measure bullying through police reports or students' sense of online bullying, not where or when the conflict happens. That matters for the part of your claim about *where* conflict goes.
- One study found students' total phone use barely changed even though use during school dropped. That raises the question of what happens to the hours outside school.
