**Peer-reviewed research**

1. **Peer-reviewed article.** Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122*(26), e2422633122. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
   - **What it found:** A field experiment with nearly 1,000 high school math students compared a plain ChatGPT-style tool ("GPT Base") with a tutor built to protect learning ("GPT Tutor"). Both tools raised practice scores. When access was taken away, GPT Base students did 17% worse on the exam than students who never had the tool. The tutor version largely avoided that drop.
   - **What I could read:** The PNAS page blocked me, so this comes from the abstract on [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html).
   - **Watch for:** PNAS issued a correction in August 2025. I couldn't read it, so check what it changed.

2. **Peer-reviewed article.** Fan, Y., Tang, L., Le, H., Shen, K., Tan, S., Zhao, Y., Shen, Y., Li, X., & Gašević, D. (2025). Beware of metacognitive laziness: Effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning motivation, processes, and performance. *British Journal of Educational Technology, 56*(2), 489–530. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13544
   - **What it found:** A randomized experiment with 117 university students on a writing task. The ChatGPT group improved their essays most, but their knowledge gain and transfer were no different from the other groups. The authors suggest the tool can encourage dependence, which they call "metacognitive laziness."
   - **Watch for:** The participants were university students, not high schoolers.

3. **Peer-reviewed article.** Deng, R., Jiang, M., Yu, X., Lu, Y., & Liu, S. (2025). Does ChatGPT enhance student learning? A systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental studies. *Computers & Education, 227*, 105224. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105224
   - **What it found:** It pooled 69 experimental studies from 2022–2024 and reports that ChatGPT use was linked to better academic performance and motivation, and to lower mental effort.
   - **What I could read:** I couldn't open the article. This summary comes from a university teaching-center page, so I don't know whether it breaks results down by school level.

4. **Peer-reviewed article (accepted, pre-proof).** Lawson, A. P., Martella, A. M., Weidlich, J., Mulders, M., & Buchner, J. (2026). Color me confounded: A critical analysis of media comparisons on ChatGPT in education. *Computers & Education, 254*, 105701. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2026.105701
   - **What it found:** The authors re-examined the studies inside the Deng meta-analysis and one other. Many compared ChatGPT groups with control groups that differed in more than just the tool, such as teaching method or time spent. They conclude that the reported benefits "must be interpreted with caution."

**Working papers and preprints (not peer reviewed)**

5. **Working paper, not peer reviewed.** Strömberg, D., Lei, V., & Wu, Y. (2026). *The generative AI learning penalty: Evidence from Chinese secondary education* (CEPR Discussion Paper No. 21577). Centre for Economic Policy Research. https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/21577.html
   - **What it found:** It tracked 26,811 students in grades 7–12 for 30 months. After students started using AI, homework scores rose 18% and monthly exam scores fell 20%. The losses were concentrated among users whose very fast homework times and high scores suggest outsourcing.
   - **Watch for:** It's observational, not randomized, so it can't prove cause as cleanly as an experiment. I read only the abstract.

6. **Working paper, not peer reviewed.** Contractor, Z., & Reyes, G. (2026). *Experimental evidence on the learning impact of generative AI* (IZA Discussion Paper No. 18792). https://docs.iza.org/dp18792.pdf
   - **What it found:** A proctored randomized experiment with undergraduates. AI access raised immediate test scores by 0.27 standard deviations, and the gain held a week later. Students who used AI to explain concepts kept their essay gains. Students who had it generate text did not.
   - **Watch for:** Undergraduates, one college, and early work the authors say may be revised.

7. **Preprint, not yet peer reviewed.** Lehmann, M., Cornelius, P. B., & Sting, F. J. (2025). *AI meets the classroom: When do large language models harm learning?* arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047
   - **What it found:** Two pre-registered lab experiments found no overall effect of LLMs on learning. Further analysis suggests that students who used LLMs to generate solutions covered more topics but understood each one less. Students who asked for explanations understood more.
   - **Watch for:** Participants came from a German university's lab pool. The usage findings come from exploratory analyses, which are weaker evidence than the main tests.

8. **Preprint, not yet peer reviewed.** LearnLM Team, Eedi, Wang, A., Rysbek, A., Huber, A., Nambiar, A., Kenolty, A., et al. (2025). *AI tutoring can safely and effectively support students: An exploratory RCT in UK classrooms.* arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23633
   - **What it found:** A randomized trial with 165 students in five UK secondary schools. An AI math tutor, supervised by human tutors, did at least as well as human tutors alone, and its students were 5.5 percentage points more likely to solve new problems.
   - **Watch for:** The team that built the tool (LearnLM, with the tutoring platform Eedi) ran the study, so the authors have a stake in a positive result. The author list is long, so copy it in full from the arXiv page.

**Research organization reports**

9. **Research organization report.** McClain, C., Anderson, M., Sidoti, O., & Bishop, W. (2026, February 24). *How teens use and view AI.* Pew Research Center. https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/
   - **What it found:** 54% of U.S. teens say they've used chatbots for help with schoolwork, and one in ten say they do all or most of their schoolwork with a chatbot's help.

10. **Research organization report.** Schwartz, H. L., & Diliberti, M. K. (2026). *More students use AI for homework, and more believe it harms critical thinking: Selected findings from the American Youth Panel* (RR-A4742-1). RAND. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4742-1.html

## Questions these results raise

- The studies sort students' use in different ways ("explanations vs. solutions," "augmentation vs. automation," fast homework times). It's worth deciding whether any of these really matches what you mean by "checking work."
- Two of the studies suggest tool design (guardrails, tutor-style prompts) matters as much as student choice. That could affect where your argument puts responsibility.
- Most of the strongest experiments use college students, so you'll need to decide how far their results can stretch to high schoolers.
