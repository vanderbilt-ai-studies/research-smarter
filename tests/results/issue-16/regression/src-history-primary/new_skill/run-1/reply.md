   This ran two days after the Liberty Loan parade. Citing the Bureau of Vital Statistics and naval doctors, it reports that the epidemic "seems to have reached its crest" and that new cases are falling. The same page notes that Camden schools have closed.
3. **Primary source.** *Evening Public Ledger* (Philadelphia). Front page. October 10, 1918, Night Extra edition, 1. [Link](https://panewsarchive.psu.edu/lccn/sn83045211/1918-10-10/ed-1/seq-1/)
   The front page reports 3,357 new cases in a day, described as a decrease. It also reports prisoners from the House of Correction digging graves, temporary burial plots for flu victims, the Phipps Institute turned into an emergency hospital, and hundreds of calls for nurses.

## Questions the results raise

- The *Ledger* published the federal health service's symptom warnings before the parade. You'd need to decide whether that counts as playing down the danger or as warning people.
