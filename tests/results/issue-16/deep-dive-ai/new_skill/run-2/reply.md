## Peer-reviewed articles (5, which covers your "at least 3 peer-reviewed")

1. **Peer-reviewed article** · Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122*(26), Article e2422633122. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
   - **What it is:** a field experiment with nearly 1,000 high school math students. One group had a plain ChatGPT-style tool; another had a tutor version with built-in safeguards. While they had access, practice grades rose 48% with the plain tool and 127% with the tutor. Once access was removed, the plain-tool group scored 17% lower than students who never had it, and the tutor's safeguards largely prevented that drop.
   - **Watch for:** the journal later published a correction. I couldn't read it, so check what it changed.

2. **Peer-reviewed article** · Fan, Y., Tang, L., Le, H., Shen, K., Tan, S., Zhao, Y., Shen, Y., Li, X., & Gašević, D. (2024). Beware of metacognitive laziness: Effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning motivation, processes, and performance. *British Journal of Educational Technology, 56*(2), 489–530. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13544
   - **What it is:** a randomized experiment with 117 university students on a writing task. Students had ChatGPT, a human expert, a writing-analytics tool, or no extra help. The ChatGPT group improved their essay scores the most, but their knowledge gain and transfer were no different from the other groups. The authors warn about "metacognitive laziness."
   - **Watch for:** the participants were university students, not high schoolers.

3. **Peer-reviewed article** · Kestin, G., Miller, K., Klales, A., Milbourne, T., & Ponti, G. (2025). AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: An RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting. *Scientific Reports, 15*, Article 17458. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6
   - **What it is:** a randomized controlled trial with college physics students. Students learned significantly more, in less time, from a custom AI tutor built on teaching best practices than from an active-learning class.
   - **Watch for:** the participants were college students, and the tool was a purpose-built tutor rather than a general chatbot.

4. **Peer-reviewed article** · Deng, R., Jiang, M., Yu, X., Lu, Y., & Liu, S. (2025). Does ChatGPT enhance student learning? A systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental studies. *Computers & Education, 227*, Article 105224. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105224
   - **What it is:** a meta-analysis, which pools the results of many experiments on ChatGPT and learning.
   - **Watch for:** I confirmed the journal and DOI, but the publisher's page blocked me and I couldn't read the abstract. I'm not describing its findings until you or I have read it.

5. **Peer-reviewed article** · Barcaui, A. (2025). ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch: Evidence from a randomized controlled trial on knowledge retention. *Social Sciences & Humanities Open, 12*, Article 102287. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssaho.2025.102287

## Working papers and preprints (not yet peer reviewed)

6. **Working paper, not peer reviewed** · Strömberg, D., Lei, V., & Wu, Y. (2026). *The generative AI learning penalty: Evidence from Chinese secondary education* [Working paper]. SSRN. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6977138
   - **What it is:** 30 months of school records for 26,811 students in grades 7–12. After students started using AI, homework scores rose 18%, but monthly exam scores fell 20% within six months. Most of the losses came from users whose very short homework times paired with high scores suggest outsourcing. AI users who spent about as long on homework as non-users had small losses.
   - **Why it's here:** it's large and real-world, and it's about secondary school.
   - **Watch for:** it isn't peer reviewed yet, and students chose for themselves whether to use AI rather than being randomly assigned.

7. **Preprint, not peer reviewed** · Dai, X., Wen, Z., Jiang, J., Liu, H., & Zhang, Y. (2025). *How students use AI feedback matters: Experimental evidence on physics achievement and autonomy* [Preprint]. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08672
   - **What it is:** two five-week randomized trials with 387 high school physics students. In one, AI hints were required; in the other, students asked for help when they wanted it. Effects differed by achievement level. Low achievers improved with required hints while medium achievers declined. On-demand help raised high achievers' scores but lowered low achievers' autonomy.
   - **Why it's here:** it's a rare randomized study of high schoolers.
   - **Watch for:** it's a preprint, and it compares students by achievement level rather than by how they used the tool.

8. **Preprint, not peer reviewed** · Bartoš, F., Martinková, P., & Wagenmakers, E.-J. (2025). *Adjusting for publication bias reveals no evidence for the effect of ChatGPT on students' learning performance, learning perception, and higher-order thinking* [Preprint]. OSF Preprints. https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/8vs32_v1
   - **What it is:** a re-analysis of a widely cited 2025 meta-analysis that had reported a large positive effect of ChatGPT on learning. Once the authors corrected for publication bias (studies with positive results are more likely to get published), the evidence of benefit disappeared.
   - **Watch for:** the meta-analysis it critiques (Wang & Fan, 2025) has since been **retracted**, so don't cite that one.
