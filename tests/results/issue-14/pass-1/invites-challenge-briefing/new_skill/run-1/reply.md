## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Is it worth it?** The schedule saves districts about 2% of their budgets, roughly $300–350 per student, mostly on buses, food service and building costs. Studies argue over whether that is enough to justify the effects on learning ([NWEA](https://www.nwea.org/blog/2023/what-the-research-tells-us-about-four-day-school-weeks/), [Thompson](https://www.educationnext.org/?p=49713460)).
- **Does it help keep teachers?** In Oregon, teacher turnover went *up* after districts switched ([FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/research-notes-does-a-four-day-school-week-improve-teacher-retention/)). Colorado found no change ([Bowser](https://edworkingpapers.com/policy-practice-series/ai25-1208)). Arkansas found it may keep teachers from moving to other schools, possibly at nearby districts' expense ([Camp](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-971)).
- **What happens on the fifth day?** People disagree about childcare, meals and supervision.

**Major voices**
- **RAND Corporation's *Does Four Equal Five?* (2021):** the large multi-state study ([summary page](https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/does-four-equal-five-implementation-and-outcomes-of-the-four-day-school-week/)). I couldn't read the full report, only summaries of it.
- **Economist Paul Thompson:** has done much of the work on Oregon.
- **Emily Morton:** an NWEA researcher and one of the RAND authors.
- **EdWorkingPapers:** a growing set of working papers on teachers. Working papers aren't peer-reviewed yet.

**Patterns**
- **Rural districts:** about 90% of four-day districts are rural. As of 2024–25, more than 2,100 schools in 26 states used the schedule ([NWEA](https://www.nwea.org/blog/2023/what-the-research-tells-us-about-four-day-school-weeks/)).
- **Test scores:** scores usually keep rising, but more slowly than in five-day districts. Most of the gap shows up where the week has fewer than about 30 hours of class time.
- **What people believe vs. what the data shows:** RAND found that people in these communities believed attendance, school climate and student behavior got better. The data mostly didn't back that up. Some administrators expected savings of 4–12%, and RAND said that wasn't possible ([Idaho Education News](https://www.idahoednews.org/top-news/a-deep-dive-into-four-day-schools-and-researchers-found-contradictions/)).
- **Satisfaction:** students and families still tend to like the schedule.

**Open questions**
- How districts actually make the decision locally
- Effects on bus drivers, cooks and other support staff
- Why some districts switch back to five days
- How kids spend the day off

Of everything here, the 2% savings figure matters most to your hunch. It's an average across studies, and one Montana study I found in search results (but couldn't open) reportedly found no savings at all. Ask me how solid that 2% figure is and I'll walk you through it.
