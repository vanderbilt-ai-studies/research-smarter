So I checked it just now. The research is **mixed and leans somewhat negative.** That's well short of "four-day weeks hurt learning." Here's what I found:

- **RAND, *Can Four Equal Five?* (Kilburn et al., 2021).** It used data from five states. Scores didn't drop after the switch, but they grew more slowly than in comparable five-day districts. After eight years the gap was about 0.14–0.23 standard deviations, which the authors call meaningful. The same brief found that parents strongly preferred the schedule (about 84% in one survey), and that leaders saw it as helping them recruit and keep teachers. [RAND brief](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RBA373-1.html)
- **The Journalist's Resource summarizes the peer-reviewed studies (Ordway, 2025):**
  - Morton, Thompson & Kuhfeld (2024) found smaller gains overall, with real declines in non-rural schools and little or no effect in rural ones.
  - Thompson & Ward (2022) found losses only in schools that cut total weekly hours the most.
  - Morton (2022) found no detectable effect on ACT scores in Oklahoma, but did find less fighting and bullying.
  - Anderson & Walker (2015) found *positive* results in Colorado elementary schools.
