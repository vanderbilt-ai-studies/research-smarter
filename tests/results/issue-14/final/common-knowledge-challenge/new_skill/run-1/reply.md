**On "common knowledge":** I shouldn't have said that, and I'm taking it back. It came from my training, not from anything I checked, and "it's common knowledge" isn't evidence. Good habit to ask that.

I just looked at the research, and it doesn't support a flat "four-day weeks hurt learning":

- **The largest recent study finds small losses, mostly outside rural areas.** Morton, Thompson and Kuhfeld followed individual students across several states. Students on four-day weeks gained a bit less over the school year, about 0.06 standard deviations in both math and reading. The losses were bigger in non-rural schools, and rural students did about as well as their five-day peers. I'm fairly confident in this because I read the paper's own summary page.
- **Instructional time seems to matter more than the number of days.** Thompson and Ward studied 12 states. Districts that cut the most hours saw small drops in scores. Districts that kept middle or high hours showed no change.
- **Some studies find no harm or even gains.** Two examples are a Colorado study (Anderson and Walker, 2015) and an Oregon kindergarten study (Thompson et al., 2023). An Oklahoma study (Morton, 2022) found no effect on ACT scores but fewer fights and bullying incidents.
