So I checked the research just now. The evidence is more mixed and more conditional than "four-day weeks hurt learning":

- **Small negative effects on average in the largest study.** Morton, Thompson and Kuhfeld followed student-level test data across several states. They found students on four-day weeks did slightly worse in reading (about −0.07 standard deviations, which is small) and grew a bit less in math and reading over the year. The effects were much larger in non-rural schools than rural ones. I read the [working-paper version](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai22-630). According to the [Journalist's Resource summary](https://journalistsresource.org/education/four-day-school-week-research/), it was later published in *Economics of Education Review* (2024), but I haven't opened the journal version.
- **It depends on how much class time is cut.** According to that same summary, Thompson and Ward's 12-state study found test scores dropped only in districts with low total time in school (about 30 hours a week). Districts that kept more time showed no change.
- **Some studies find no harm.** The summary also reports a Colorado study (Anderson and Walker, 2015) that linked four-day weeks to *better* elementary reading and math scores, and an Oregon study that found little difference for kindergartners.
- **The University of Oregon's HEDCO review** of 11 studies reportedly found "no evidence of large positive effects," with results that vary by location and grade. I couldn't read the PDF itself, so I only know this secondhand from the summary.

**How confident I am:** I'm fairly confident the overall pattern is "small average harm, mostly where time is cut, not universal." I've only read summaries of most of these studies, not the full papers.
