## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Do students learn less?** RAND's 2021 study, the largest so far, covered six states. Eight years after a switch, test scores in four-day districts were about 0.14–0.23 standard deviations lower than they would otherwise have been. A University of Oregon review of 11 studies (2025) found no large positive effects on any outcome. A Missouri state analysis (2024) found no significant difference.
- **Does it help schools hire and keep teachers?** That's the main reason districts give. Educators widely believe it works. A recent CALDER study of Missouri teacher data from 2009–2024 found no evidence that it improved recruitment or turnover. A study of Texas districts found turnover fell by about 2.7 percentage points.
- **Does it save money?** Districts sometimes expect savings of up to 20%. Research estimates are closer to 0.4–2.5% of the budget, because salaries don't change.

**Major voices**
RAND Corporation; Paul Thompson (Oregon State); Emily Morton (Oklahoma studies); Cory Koedel and the CALDER Center (Missouri); the University of Oregon's HEDCO Institute; the National Conference of State Legislatures (state policy).

**Patterns**
- Most research is on small rural districts, mostly in Oregon, Oklahoma and Colorado. City schools are barely studied.
- **Time is key.** Four-day districts in RAND's study had 146 school days instead of 171. Some research suggests the score gap shrinks where districts keep more total instructional time.
- People's beliefs often differ from the measured results. Parents and teachers report high satisfaction (69% of elementary parents in RAND's survey would definitely choose it again), but the data on outcomes are mixed or negative.
- **Behavior findings point different ways.** An Oklahoma study found fewer bullying incidents and fights. A Colorado study found youth crime rose about 20%, mostly property crime, and rose most on Thursdays, which was the day off there.

**Open questions**
- What do students actually do on the fifth day? The Oregon review says this is unanswered.
- Effects on childcare, meals for food-insecure students, and hourly support staff are mentioned as concerns but rarely measured.
- How does it work in suburban and city districts as more of them adopt it?
