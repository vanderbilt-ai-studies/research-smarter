## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Achievement.** Do students learn less? A RAND team (Doss, Phillips and Kilburn) found that students in four-day districts fell slightly further behind each year, and that the losses added up over time. Other studies found little or no difference, especially where districts kept their weekly hours high. A Missouri state analysis found no significant difference.
- **Teachers.** Districts often switch to attract and keep teachers. The largest study so far, from Texas, found turnover fell about 2.7 percentage points, but more than 1 in 5 teachers still left each year. A Missouri working paper found no improvement in recruiting or keeping teachers.
- **Is it worth it?** Estimated savings are small, about 0.4% to 2.5% of a district's budget, and new programs for the day off can cancel them out. Parents in four-day districts, though, often report high satisfaction.

**Major voices**
- Economists and policy researchers dominate: Paul Thompson (Oregon State), Emily Morton, and RAND Corporation teams.
- State education departments in Missouri and Oklahoma have done their own analyses.
- Education Week and Journalist's Resource track the research for a general audience.

**Patterns**
- Most studies compare test scores or staff records before and after a district switches. Fewer ask students, families, or teachers about their experience.
- Results depend on context: how many hours a district keeps, and whether it's rural. One large study found little impact in rural schools but declines in non-rural ones.
- Adoption is growing fast: more than 2,100 schools in 26 states as of 2024-25, mostly small and rural districts.

**Open questions**
- What students actually do on the fifth day. One Oregon study (self-reported data) linked the schedule to more food insecurity, and a Colorado study found about a 20% rise in juvenile offenses.
- How parents' jobs and childcare costs are affected. This is barely measured.
- Whether gains in keeping teachers last once nearby districts switch too.
- Whether results from small rural districts apply to the larger districts now adopting the schedule, as in Texas.
