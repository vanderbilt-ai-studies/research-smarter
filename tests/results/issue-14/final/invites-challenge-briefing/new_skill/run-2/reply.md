## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Why districts switch.** RAND's 2021 brief calls saving money the most important factor in district decisions. A 2023 NWEA overview by Emily Morton (updated in 2026) lists teacher recruitment and retention, cost savings and attendance as the reasons leaders give. Some leaders keep the schedule even after finding the savings smaller than they expected.
- **Whether it saves much.** People often assume that cutting one day in five saves up to 20%. Salaries and benefits don't shrink with a shorter week, though, so measured savings average about 2% of budgets. Those savings come mostly from buses, food service and operations.
- **Whether it helps hire and keep teachers.** An Arkansas study found teachers in four-day districts were about 28% more likely to stay. The advantage was largest when no nearby district also had a four-day week. Statewide studies in Oregon and Montana found no difference.

**Major voices**
- RAND Corporation's multi-state study, *Can Four Equal Five?* (2021)
- Emily Morton, whose peer-reviewed Oklahoma finance and achievement study is in *Educational Researcher*
- The University of Oregon's HEDCO Institute, which published a systematic review of 11 studies in 2025
- Education Week, which keeps reporting on new studies

**Patterns**
- Most four-day districts are rural, about 90% according to NWEA, so most of the evidence comes from rural schools.
- Achievement results lean negative. RAND found students in four-day districts still improved, but more slowly than they would have on five days, and the gap grew over eight years. The NWEA overview reports that most of the harm shows up in districts with fewer than about 30 instructional hours a week.
- Parents and students like the schedule a lot. In RAND's survey of 766 elementary-school parents, 84% said they would definitely or probably choose the four-day week.

**Open questions**
- HEDCO names two: how much instructional time is actually lost, and what students do on the day off.
- Several sources note gaps on childcare, meals, safety and effects that differ by family income.
