## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Why districts switch.** The National Conference of State Legislatures says cutting costs is the most common reason. Attracting and keeping teachers is second, especially in rural districts. Some districts now promote the schedule mainly as a perk for teachers.
- **Whether it saves much money.** Districts still pay teachers the same, so savings come from buses, food, utilities, and support staff. NCSL puts the theoretical maximum at about 5.4% of the budget and typical savings at 0.4–2.5%.
- **Whether learning suffers.** In Oregon, Paul Thompson found math and reading scores dropped, and the drop grew in schools that stayed on four days. RAND found scores kept rising, but more slowly than they would have on a five-day week. An early Colorado study found gains.

**Major voices**
- Economists such as Paul Thompson (Oregon State).
- The RAND Corporation and NWEA (Emily Morton and colleagues).
- Teacher-workforce researchers, such as Ainsworth, Penner, and Liu (2024).
- NCSL and state education agencies.

**Patterns**
- **Surveys and records disagree.** Satisfaction surveys and interviews are very positive. In RAND's survey, 85% of parents and 95% of students would keep the schedule. Studies using district records are more mixed or negative.
- **Teacher turnover.** Administrators often say the schedule helps hold onto teachers. Oregon district records showed turnover went *up* 2.3 percentage points after districts switched.
- **Instructional time.** Results seem to depend on whether districts keep total weekly hours high, and on whether the district is rural.

**Open questions**
- Effects outside school are less studied. One peer-reviewed Colorado study (Fischer and Argyle, 2018) linked the schedule to a roughly 20% rise in youth crime, mostly property crime. Child care, parents' jobs, and meals on the day off have mostly been raised as concerns, with little research measuring them so far.
- Why districts switch back to five days is barely researched.
- How well rural findings apply to the suburban districts now trying the schedule is still unclear.
