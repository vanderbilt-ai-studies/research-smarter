## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Achievement.** An early Colorado study found small gains. RAND's work across several states found students improved more slowly. A multi-state NWEA/Oregon State study found harm in small-town and suburban districts but little effect in rural ones ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-seven-new-studies-on-the-impact-of-a-four-day-school-week/); [EdWeek, 2026](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/the-4-day-school-week-what-research-shows-about-the-alternative-schedule/2026/04)).
- **Money.** Districts most often say they switch to cut costs ([NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview)). However, an Education Commission of the States analysis, cited by EdWeek, NCSL and AP, puts actual savings at about 0.4–2.5% of a district's budget. Programs for the day off can eat into that.
- **Teachers.** Independence, Missouri reported a large jump in job applicants after switching ([KCUR](https://www.kcur.org/education/2024-10-21/independence-attracted-more-teachers-thanks-to-4-day-school-a-missouri-law-could-end-it)). Statewide turnover studies in Missouri and Texas find small or no effects on whether teachers stay.

**Major voices**
- Economist Paul Thompson (Oregon State) and researcher Emily Morton (NWEA) appear across much of the achievement and cost research.
- RAND did the big multi-state study ([brief, Kilburn et al., 2021](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_briefs/RBA300/RBA373-1/RAND_RBA373-1.pdf)). I could open the PDF but couldn't read its text, so I've only confirmed the title and authors.
- Missouri's PRiME Center (Jon Turner) studies teachers. NCSL and the Education Commission of the States track policy.

**Patterns**
- Most four-day districts are small and rural, so a lot of the evidence comes from rural places. Some larger districts, like Independence, have switched recently.
- Two adoption waves: after the 2008 recession, then again after the pandemic. EdWeek counts more than 2,100 schools in 26 states in 2024-25.
- Whether a district keeps total instructional hours (by lengthening the other four days) seems to matter a lot for achievement results.
- Families report high satisfaction in surveys, while child care on the off day is a common complaint ([PBS/AP, 2023](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/education/more-school-districts-adopt-4-day-weeks-citing-lower-costs-and-better-teacher-recruitment)).

**Open questions**
- **Spreading to larger districts.** Will results from rural studies hold in suburban and urban ones?
- **Effects beyond test scores.** A Colorado study linked the switch to about a 20% rise in juvenile crime ([Fischer & Argyle, 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecoedu/v64y2018icp31-39.html)). An Oklahoma study found less bullying.
- **Costs to families.** Little research measures what families pay, such as child care and lost work, on the off day.
- **Teacher hiring.** Does the edge last once neighboring districts switch too?
