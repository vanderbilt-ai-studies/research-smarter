I checked your point about class time, and it mostly holds up, with one detail you'll need. Districts on four-day weeks usually make each school day longer. RAND's 2021 *Does Four Equal Five?* study still found that, on average, students got fewer instructional hours over the year, and their test scores grew more slowly than they would have on a five-day week ([RAND brief](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_briefs/RBA300/RBA373-1/RAND_RBA373-1.pdf)). A related study by Thompson and Ward found the harm to math scores showed up mainly in districts that cut total time the most, so the size of the loss depends on how a district sets up its schedule. The same RAND work also found that families and students said they really valued the extra day together.

That last finding is where a thoughtful opponent would start. What would you need to find out to know that's true?
