## What I found and didn't find

- **Plenty of evidence** that bans cut phone use during school but not total phone use (sources 2 and 7), and studies of bullying in general point in different directions.
- **Thin:** in-person interaction measured directly. The evidence comes from what students and staff report (6, 7, 9). No study I found observed students at lunch or between classes, and source 1 says outright that it didn't measure this.
- **Not found:** any study measuring cyberbullying by *time of day* (during school vs. after school), before and after a ban.
- **Age:** several studies cover middle schoolers or ages 12–15 (2, 4), not just high schoolers.

## Questions these results raise

- Which way conflict moves: source 7 describes after-school online disputes ending up *at* school, which is a different direction from the one your position describes.
- Whether "less bullying reported" means less bullying or less of it being *seen* (sources 9 and 10).
- How far you can rely on results from schools where most high schoolers don't follow the ban (source 9).
