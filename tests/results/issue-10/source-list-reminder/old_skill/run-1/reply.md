## What I found and didn't find

- **Plenty:** wellbeing, phone time, and bullying measured as a general outcome.
- **Thin:** in-person interaction measured directly. I found no peer-reviewed study that counts or observes how much students actually talk face to face. The evidence on that comes from what students say about it (sources 2 and 9).
- **Not found:** any study that tracks *where or when* bullying happens before and after a ban. The "moves to after school" idea shows up in students' own accounts and in the compensation pattern in phone time, but nobody has measured it directly.
- **Watch the ages:** several samples include 12–15-year-olds, and your question is about high school students.

## Questions the results raise

- Researchers measure "interaction" in different ways: how much students talk, how connected they feel to school, and how much they feel they belong. Sources 2 and 5 don't measure the same thing.
- Students' views of bans, teachers' views, and measured outcomes don't always match. That affects how much weight each kind of source can carry.
