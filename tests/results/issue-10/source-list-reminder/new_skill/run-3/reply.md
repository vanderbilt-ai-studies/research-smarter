**Peer-reviewed studies**

1. Vereecke, J., Vanherle, R., Nassen, L.-M., Boen, H., Eggermont, S., Beullens, K., Vandenbosch, L., & Schreurs, L. (2026). More than disconnecting? School smartphone bans and adolescents' social wellbeing. *Frontiers in Developmental Psychology, 4*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fdpys.2026.1796471
   *One Belgian secondary school (960 students, average age about 15) moved from a loose restriction to a total ban. Students were surveyed monthly for eight months.    *This study compared 1,398 students at 24 Dutch schools that had either a classroom-only ban or a full-day ban. It found no significant differences in well-being or bullying. **Working papers (not yet peer-reviewed)**

9. Allcott, H., Baron, E. J., Dee, T., Duckworth, A. L., Gentzkow, M., & Jacob, B. (2026). *The effects of school phone bans: National evidence from lockable pouches* (NBER Working Paper No. 35132). National Bureau of Economic Research. https://www.nber.org/papers/w35132

## What I found and didn't find

- **Plenty:** studies on bullying and cyberbullying, and on well-being. The results point in different directions.
- **Thin:** direct measurement of face-to-face interaction. Nearly all of it is self-report, either students saying they talk more or teacher surveys. I found no peer-reviewed study that actually observed conversation at lunch or between classes.
- **Missing:** a study that checks *when* conflict happens. The cyberbullying studies measure it overall and don't separate school hours from after school. The closest thing is the Goodyear and Weiss finding that *phone time* moves outside school.
- **Who's studied:** many samples are middle schoolers or Europeans. Only Chen and Allcott clearly include high schools.

## Questions the results raise

- Do "less phone time at school" and "conflict moving to after school" describe the same mechanism, or two different claims that need different evidence?
- Several studies find bans have *no* effect on cyberbullying. Your position predicts *no drop* in cyberbullying too, so a null result doesn't by itself separate your view from "bans just don't matter much."
