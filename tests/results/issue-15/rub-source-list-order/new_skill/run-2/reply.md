1. **Peer-reviewed article** (*PNAS*). Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122*(26), Article e2422633122. Abstract: https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html · Penn listing: https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/generative-ai-without-guardrails-can-harm-learning-evidence-from-high-school-mathematics

**Preprints and working papers** (not yet peer reviewed. They're the closest matches to your "how they use it" sub-question, but treat their findings as provisional)

4. **Preprint, not yet peer reviewed.** Lehmann, M., Cornelius, P. B., & Sting, F. J. (2025). *AI meets the classroom: When do large language models harm learning?* (arXiv:2409.09047) [Preprint]. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047
   Two pre-registered lab experiments found no overall effect of LLMs on learning. Further analyses suggest it depends on use: students who used them to *substitute* for work (like generating solutions) covered more topics but understood each one less, while students who used them to *complement* learning (like asking for explanations) understood more. *Why it's here:* it splits students by type of use directly. *Watch for:* the abstract doesn't say how old the students were, and the use-type findings are labeled exploratory.

5. **Preprint, not yet peer reviewed.** Rismanchian, S., Uzun, H., Matayoshi, J., Cosyn, E., & Kurd-Misto, E. (2026). *Faster completion, less learning: Generative AI reduced study time on math problems and the knowledge they build* (arXiv:2605.21629) [Preprint]. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21629
   Looks at 3.2 million interactions on the ALEKS math platform. After ChatGPT came out, time spent on word problems (easy for AI) fell 31.3% among high schoolers compared with graph problems (hard for AI). Among college students, accuracy on proctored retention questions declined. *Why it's here:* large-scale, includes high schoolers. *Watch for:* the authors couldn't see whether students were actually using AI, and some co-authors work for the company that makes ALEKS.

6. **Working paper, not yet peer reviewed.** Strömberg, D., Lei, V., & Wu, Y. *The generative AI learning penalty: Evidence from Chinese secondary education* (CEPR Discussion Paper No. 21577). *Found in search results. ## What I found and didn't find

- **Plenty:** studies where students use AI on tasks and get tested later, and surveys on how many teens use it.
- **Thin:** high school-specific evidence. Only #1, #5, and #6 focus on high schoolers. #1 is a single school in Turkey, and #5 and #6 are observational and not yet peer reviewed. Most experiments use university students.
- **Not found:** any study that measures "checking your work" as its own category. The closest are *substitute vs. complement* (#4), *tutor vs. open chatbot* (#1), and *homework time* (#6). I also found no U.S. high school randomized trial and very little long-term evidence.
- **Caution:** search results say a widely cited 2025 meta-analysis (Wang & Fan, *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*) was retracted in April 2026. I couldn't open the notice, so check the status if you come across it.
