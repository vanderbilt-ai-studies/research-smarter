## What I found and didn't find

- **Easy to find:** archives of Philadelphia newspapers, and broad histories of the epidemic in the city.
- **Thin:** peer-reviewed work on *how Philadelphia papers covered* the flu. I found none written specifically on that, and no study of how the papers' tone changed over time. That gap may be your paper's opening. It also means most of your evidence for the change in tone will come from reading the papers yourself.
- **Not found:** records showing the federal government directly censored Philadelphia's flu coverage. The articles I found on wartime censorship were popular press pieces, so I left them out.

## Questions these results raise

- Whether the papers all moved together, or whether one paper started warning readers earlier than the others.
- The Higgins title suggests historians may have cast Krusen as a villain too easily. That could affect how you read the officials' statements that the papers printed.
- If the scrapbook's unknown compiler chose what went in, it may show one view of the coverage rather than all of it.
