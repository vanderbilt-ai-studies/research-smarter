**Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction (exit check)**

Welcome back. Last time you picked the angle "sleep and how well people think," because you're always tired during exams and wanted to know whether it matters. The step left was writing your draft research question, and you've now done that.

There's a lot to like in your draft. It's specific: one intervention (a 15-minute nap), one outcome (focus), one population (adults), and one time window (ten minutes). Most first drafts are far vaguer, so this is a solid start. Two things are worth looking at before you move on:

1. **Scope against the assignment.** This is an 8-page literature review, so your question has to be answerable from what other people have already published. The narrower the conditions, the fewer studies will match all of them. "Exactly 15 minutes" plus "within ten minutes" is a very small window. If you only found a handful of studies that tested those exact conditions, would that be enough to organize a whole review around?

2. **What "focus" means.** Researchers measure attention and alertness in different ways. Some use how sleepy people say they feel, some use reaction-time tasks, and some use sustained attention tests. A reader of your review will want to know which of these counts.

Start with the first one, since it affects everything else: what would you do if the evidence for those exact conditions turned out to be thin?
