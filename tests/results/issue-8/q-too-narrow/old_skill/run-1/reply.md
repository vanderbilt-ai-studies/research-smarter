> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. You finished intake for your 8-page psychology literature review, which is due May 20, 2027. On November 14 you chose the angle "sleep and how well people think" because you're always tired during exams. You were about to write your draft research question, and now you have one, so we're at the Phase 1 exit check.

Your question has real strengths. It names a specific intervention (a 15-minute nap), a population (adults), an outcome (focus) and a time window (ten minutes). Most first drafts are much vaguer. Because it's so concrete, it's also easy to see where it might strain.

The main thing to look at is how well it fits the assignment. A literature review maps a whole conversation across many studies. Your question is phrased as yes or no, and it's narrowed four ways at once. That raises two questions for you:

1. **If you found the answer, would it fill eight pages?** Or would it be one finding you could state in a paragraph? Think about which parts of the question (the 15 minutes, the ten-minute window, "focus") you'd be willing to loosen, and which ones are the heart of what you care about.

2. **How does a nap connect to why you picked this angle?** You picked it because of exam-season tiredness, and the step from that to a nap's effect in the first ten minutes isn't clear yet. What's the link for you?

You don't need to answer both. Start with whichever one feels more useful.
