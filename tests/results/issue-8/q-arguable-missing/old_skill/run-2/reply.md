> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for your 8-page argumentative paper in education policy. Then you chose the angle of how four-day school weeks are set up, because your cousin's district just switched to one. Now you've written a draft question, which is the Phase 1 exit check.

Your question ties directly to the angle you picked, and it's clear what it's about. The main problem is that it doesn't yet fit an argumentative paper. "What are the main features" has a mostly factual answer: you could list how districts schedule the days, what happens on the fifth day, and how the hours get made up, and there'd be nothing for a reader to disagree with. Your paper needs a thesis someone could reasonably push back on, so the question has to leave room for more than one defensible answer. "Main features" is also broad: across all districts, everywhere, judged by whom?

You don't have to drop the "how it's set up" angle. Setup choices can be very arguable, because some designs might work better than others, for some students more than others. That direction is yours to find, though, so here's the question to work from:

**When you think about the way your cousin's district set up its four-day week, what part of it could thoughtful people disagree about?**

Once you have an answer, try rewriting your draft question around it, and we'll check it again.
