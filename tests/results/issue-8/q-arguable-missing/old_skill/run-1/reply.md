> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you picked the angle of how four-day school weeks are set up, because your cousin's district just switched to one. You've now written a draft research question, which is the last step of Phase 1.

Your question has real strengths. It's clear, someone else would understand what you're asking, and you'll be able to find plenty of evidence for it. Your cousin's district also gives you a real case to look at up close.

The weak spot is that this is an argumentative paper, and you'll need to defend a thesis for 8 pages. Your question asks for a description. A good answer to "what are the main features" would be a list: which day gets dropped, how long the other days run, and so on. Two careful researchers would probably come up with much the same list, so there's not much for your paper to take a side on.

Here's what I'd like you to think about: **if your paper answered this question, what claim could someone reasonably disagree with?** If you can't find one yet, think about what you'd *want* to argue about how these schedules are set up. That will point you toward a sharper version, and you'll still be the one who writes it.
