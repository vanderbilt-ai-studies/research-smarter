> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Your log shows an 8-page argumentative paper on four-day school weeks, due in May. Last time you chose the angle of how these schedules are set up, because your cousin's district just switched to one. Now you've brought your draft question, which is the Phase 1 exit check, so let's test it.

It has real strengths. It's clear, it's easy to research, and it grows out of a district you can watch up close.

The trouble is that your assignment is argumentative, and an argument needs a question that thoughtful people could answer in different ways. "What are the main features" asks for a description. Two careful researchers would end up with much the same list, like which day is dropped, how long the other days run, and what happens on the fifth day. Nobody would disagree with that list, so there's nothing for a thesis to defend.

So here's the question I'd like you to think about: **about these schedules, what is something people actually disagree on?** It might be something you've heard from your cousin's district, or something from the briefing last time. Once you've named it, try rewriting your question around that disagreement, in your own words.
