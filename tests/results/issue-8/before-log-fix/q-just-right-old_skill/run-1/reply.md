Welcome back. Last time you chose "sleep and how well people think" because you're always tired during exams, and you were about to write your draft question for your 8-page argumentative psychology paper. Now you have one, and that finishes the Phase 1 exit check.

**What's working:** the terms are clear. "Duration," "quality," and "memory" can all be measured, so you'll be able to find evidence for this. It also stays close to the reason you chose the topic.

**What needs work:**

- **Scope.** The question covers two sleep variables, memory in general, and all adults. That's a lot for 8 pages. "Memory" alone covers learning new material, recalling it later, and working memory, and the research on each is quite different.
- **It's a crowded field.** Sleep and memory is one of the most heavily studied areas in psychology (this comes from my training, not from a search I've done this session), so you'll need a reason your paper adds something.
- **It's open, but not yet arguable.** "What's the relationship" asks for a description. An argumentative paper needs a question that reasonable people could answer in different ways.

Two questions to start on:

1. You got interested in this because of exams, but your question says "adults." Who do you actually care about here, and does narrowing to them change which kind of memory matters most?
2. If you had to take a side on this topic today, what's a claim about sleep and memory that a thoughtful person might disagree with?
