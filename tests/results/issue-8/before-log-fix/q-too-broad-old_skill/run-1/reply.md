**Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. You're writing an 8-page argumentative psychology paper, due in May, and you chose the angle "sleep and how well people think" because you're always tired during exams. You've now reached the Phase 1 exit check: your draft research question. Writing it down is real progress.

Here's my honest read. Your question names the territory, but it's still the whole territory. "Sleep" could mean how long people sleep, when they sleep, naps, or pulling an all-nighter. "Cognition" could mean memory, attention, reasoning, or exam performance. Eight pages can't cover all of that well.

Since this is an argumentative paper, the bigger issue is whether the question gives you something to argue. Your thesis has to be something a thoughtful person could disagree with. A question whose obvious answer is "yes, sleep matters" leaves you nothing to defend.

Two questions to work on, in your own words:

1. Your reason for this angle was exam stress. Which kind of sleep and which kind of thinking does that experience point you toward?
2. When you picture someone reading your paper and disagreeing with you, what do you picture them disagreeing about?

Take a shot at a revised question once you've thought these through. It's fine if it's rough.
