Welcome back. Last time you chose the angle "sleep and how well people think," because you're always tired during exams and wanted to know whether it matters. You're at the Phase 1 exit check, and now you have a draft question, which is real progress.

Your question is a good place to start: it names your topic clearly and it's something researchers actually study. For an 8-page argumentative paper, though, I see two problems:

- **It's very broad.** "Sleep" could mean total hours, all-nighters, naps, or sleep timing. "Cognition" covers memory, attention, reasoning, decision-making and more. "Human" includes everyone from infants to older adults. This is one of the most-studied areas in psychology, so each of these words opens up a huge body of research.
- **It's hard to argue.** "How does X affect Y" asks for a description, and an argumentative paper needs a claim that thoughtful people could disagree with.

Your exam experience is a good way to narrow it. When you're tired during exams, which kind of thinking actually suffers, and whose sleep are you most curious about?

Once you've narrowed it, think about this too: what's one thing about sleep and thinking that people, students, or researchers might actually disagree on?
