Welcome back. Last time you chose the angle "sleep and how well people think" because you're always tired during exams and wanted to know whether that matters. You were about to write your draft research question, and now you have, so this is the Phase 1 exit check.

Your question has real strengths. It's neutral, because it doesn't assume sleep helps or hurts. It also can't be answered with a yes or no, which suits a literature review that has to organize a whole field.

The main problem is scope. "Sleep" and "cognition" are both huge. Cognition can mean memory, attention, decision-making, reasoning, creativity and more. Sleep can mean how long you sleep, how well you sleep, naps, staying up all night, or circadian timing. Each of those pairings has a large research literature of its own, and you have eight pages.

So, two questions for you:

1. When you picture yourself tired during exams, which part of your thinking feels like it suffers most?
2. With that in mind, what would you have to leave out to cover this question well in eight pages?
