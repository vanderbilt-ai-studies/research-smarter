> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you chose the angle "sleep and how well people think" because you're always tired during exams and want to know whether it matters. You were about to write your draft research question for your 8-page psychology literature review, and now you have one.

This is a strong draft. Here's what's working:

- **It's clear and specific.** "How well people think" has become **memory**, which is something you can actually measure and look up. That makes your search much easier.
- **It names two separate parts of sleep,** duration and quality. That gives a literature review a natural way to organize itself, because you can show what's known about each and where they overlap.
- **It names a population** (adults) and **it's neutral.** It doesn't assume an answer, and nobody could answer it with a yes or no.

Two things to think about before we move on:

1. Your interest started with your own exam fatigue, but the question covers all adults. When you picture the studies you'll be reading, whose sleep and whose memory do you most want to understand?
2. With eight pages and two parts of sleep, do you plan to give duration and quality equal weight, or is one of them closer to what you actually want to know?

Answer in your own words. If you change the question, rewrite it yourself. When you're happy with it, we'll move to Phase 2 and stress-test it.
