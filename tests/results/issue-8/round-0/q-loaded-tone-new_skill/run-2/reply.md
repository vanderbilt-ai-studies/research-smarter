**Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you chose the downsides of school phone bans as your angle, partly because you can't stand your own school's ban. You were about to write a draft research question, and now you have one. That's the Phase 1 exit check.

Your draft has a real strength. Your assignment is an argumentative paper, and you've clearly picked a side. That will help once you're writing.

The problem is that the question already contains its answer. "Why are they a terrible idea?" treats "terrible" as settled before you've looked at any evidence. Researching it this way would push you to collect only what agrees with you. It would also leave you unprepared for the counterargument your paper will have to answer. "Terrible" is also vague: terrible for whom, and judged by what?

**What would your question look like if you weren't sure of the answer yet?** Keep your conviction. You can still argue against bans in the end, and you'll argue it better if your question leaves room for the evidence to surprise you. Try a new version in your own words, even a rough one.
