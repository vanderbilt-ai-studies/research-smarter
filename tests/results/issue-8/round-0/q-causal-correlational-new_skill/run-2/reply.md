> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for your 8-page argumentative psychology paper. You chose the angle of whether social media causes anxiety in teens, partly because many of your friends say Instagram makes them anxious. Now you've written a draft question and a plan, which completes the Phase 1 exit check.

Your question has real strengths. It's clear, it's short, and it names a specific group (teenagers) and a specific outcome (anxiety). You could argue it either way, which fits an argumentative paper.

There's a gap between the question and your plan, though. Your question uses the word **"cause."** Your plan is surveys that compare how much teens use social media with how anxious they are. Suppose a survey finds that teens who use social media heavily also report more anxiety. What other explanations could produce that same result, besides social media causing the anxiety?

Once you've thought that through, look at the question again. Could a reader answer it with a simple "yes" or "no"? What would your eight pages need to weigh to get beyond that?
