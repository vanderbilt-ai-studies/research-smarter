> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for a 600-word opinion piece for the school paper (at least 2 credible sources, MLA, due May 20, 2027). You chose the angle of whether your school should start later, because first period is brutal. You were about to write your draft research question, and now you have.

**Your question: "Should my school start later?"** It's a good start for this assignment, and here's what's working:

- **It's arguable.** Reasonable people could say yes or no. Students, parents, teachers, bus schedulers and coaches all have a stake, so there's a real debate for an opinion piece.
- **It's focused.** Asking about *your* school, not schools in general, keeps it small enough for 600 words. It also gives you something other writers don't have: you can speak to your own school's schedule and the people in it.
- **It matters to your readers.** Everyone reading the school paper lives with the start time.

The weakest spot is **clarity**, specifically the word "later." Right now a reader can't tell what you're proposing, and that changes the whole argument. A small shift and a big one bring very different benefits and costs. Pinning this down will also make the counterargument easier to find later.

One question for you: **what would "later" actually mean at your school, and what makes you pick that amount?**

Once you've thought it through, rewrite the question in your own words if your answer changes it. Then we can move on to stress-testing it.
