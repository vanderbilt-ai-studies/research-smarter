**Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you finished intake (an 8-page argumentative sociology paper, due May 2027) and chose the downsides of school phone bans as your angle, because you hate your school's ban. The only step left in Phase 1 was writing your draft question, and now you have one.

The draft has real strengths. It's clearly arguable, and that's what a thesis-driven paper needs. You also have a stake in the topic, which will keep you going for eight pages.

My main pushback is about how the question is built. It already assumes its answer. "Why are phone bans a terrible idea?" can only be answered with reasons they're terrible. That turns the research into collecting support for a conclusion you've already reached. An argumentative paper only persuades a skeptical reader if your position could have turned out wrong and survived the evidence anyway.

"Terrible" is also doing a lot of work without a definition. Terrible for whom? Students, teachers, parents? And measured by what? Grades, wellbeing, safety, students' sense of autonomy?

Here are two things to think about before you revise:

1. If you found strong evidence that bans improved something, like focus or bullying rates, could your current question make room for it?
2. When you say "terrible," what specific harm do you have in mind?

You don't have to answer both. Start with whichever one you have a clearer reaction to, then try rewriting the question in your own words.
