**Reports, working papers, and conference abstracts (not peer-reviewed)**

9. Reynolds, M., Esfandiari, M., & O'Higgins Norman, J. (2025). *Restriction or resilience? Smartphone bans in schools: A qualitative study of the experiences of students.* DCU Anti-Bullying Centre. [DCU page](https://www.dcu.ie/antibullyingcentre/restriction-or-resilience)

## What I found and didn't find

- **Well covered:** wellbeing and academic results, and school-day bullying counts.
- **Thin:** direct measures of face-to-face interaction. Nearly all of it is self-report or qualitative: focus groups, surveys, teacher impressions. I found no study that observed or counted conversations.
- **Missing:** I found no study that tracks *when* conflict happens before and after a ban. Several sources raise the after-school question (sources 2, 7, and 9), but none measures it.
- **Age mismatch:** much of the evidence covers ages 12–15 or middle schools, not high school specifically.

## Questions these results raise

- Source 7 suggests online conflict can show up *in person at school*, which runs in a different direction from conflict moving out of school. That deserves a close look.
- Officially reported bullying (source 5) and self-reported bullying (source 3) may not measure the same thing, and they may react to a ban differently.
- Bans differ a lot, from bell-to-bell bans to classroom-only rules, so "a ban" may not be one thing.
