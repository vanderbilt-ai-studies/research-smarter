## Briefing: four-day school weeks

I searched scholarly databases and read the abstracts below. Most full texts are paywalled, so I haven't read the full papers.

**Key debates**
- **Learning:** Does a shorter week hurt learning? An early Colorado study (Anderson & Walker, 2015) found little evidence that achievement suffered. Later work found small drops. In Oregon, scores fell by about 0.03–0.06 standard deviations (Thompson, 2021). A multi-state study found about −0.07 SD in reading (Morton, Thompson & Kuhfeld, 2024). An Oklahoma study found no detectable effect on scores (Morton, 2020).
- **Teachers:** Does the schedule help hire and keep teachers? Districts often say it does. Several 2026 studies find it doesn't do so consistently. Missouri showed no retention gain (Camp, Anglum & Koedel). In Oregon, teacher turnover rose about 2 percentage points (Ainsworth et al.).
- **Costs:** Does it save money? Savings tend to come from buses, food service and operations. In Oklahoma they came to about 2% of a district's budget (Morton, 2020).

**Major voices**
- A small group of education economists does most of this work, especially Paul Thompson and Emily Morton.
- Researchers at NWEA (the testing organization) and the RAND Corporation have also contributed.
- The journal *Education Finance and Policy* publishes much of it.

**Patterns**
- Most studies use a "difference-in-differences" design, which compares districts before and after they switch with districts that didn't.
- A few states dominate the research: Oregon, Colorado, Oklahoma and Missouri.
- Adopters are mostly small and rural. Four-day schedules average about 148 days a year, compared with roughly 180 for five-day schools (Thompson, Gunter & Schuna, 2021).
- Effects may be larger in non-rural schools.

**Open questions**
- **Students' lives outside school:** An Oregon survey study linked four-day schools to more food insecurity and less PE (Tomayko et al., 2021). Juvenile property arrests rose about 73% in rural Colorado counties that switched (Fischer & Argyle, 2018).
- **Families:** Childcare and parents' work have barely been studied.
- **Teachers' own wellbeing:** Little has been published.
- **Why results vary:** Research hasn't settled why outcomes differ by state and by schedule design.
