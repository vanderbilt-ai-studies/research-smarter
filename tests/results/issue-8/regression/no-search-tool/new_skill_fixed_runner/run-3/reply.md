## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Achievement.** Early work found no harm. A 2015 study of Colorado elementary schools even found a *positive* link with reading and math scores ([Anderson & Walker](https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00165)). A later multi-state study using student-level data found small negative effects, about −0.07 SD in spring reading. Those effects were larger in non-rural schools and for girls, and may grow over time ([Morton, Thompson & Kuhfeld](https://doi.org/10.26300/p96h-8a41)).
- **Does it deliver what districts want?** A 2021 RAND study found "only weak support" for the three usual reasons districts give: saving money, fewer absences, and keeping teachers ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA373-1.html)). A new Texas working paper (October 2026, not yet peer-reviewed) found teachers 2.1 percentage points more likely to stay in their district, with no average drop in test scores ([Lohner & Weng](https://doi.org/10.26300/c16c-5283)).
- **Popularity vs. outcomes.** RAND found families and staff very satisfied with the schedule even though test scores grew more slowly.

**Major voices**
- Economists and policy researchers, with Paul Thompson and Emily Morton appearing again and again.
- The RAND Corporation.
- The Annenberg Institute's EdWorkingPapers series, where much new work appears first.

**Patterns**
- Most studies compare districts before and after they switch, using state test and budget data. Very few talk to students directly.
- Adoption is mostly small and rural and usually financially motivated. A 2021 national count found over 1,600 schools in 24 states. Those schools averaged about 148 school days a year, compared with the usual 180 ([Thompson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00316)). That count is now five years old.
- Measured savings tend to be small. One Oklahoma study put them at about 2% of the budget (Morton 2020, *Educational Researcher*).
- Adoption spreads between neighbors. In Missouri, the strongest predictor of a district switching was that nearby districts already had (Anglum & Park 2021, *AERA Open*).

**Open questions**
- Effects outside academics. In Oklahoma high schools, bullying incidents fell about 39% and fights about 31% after the switch (Morton 2022, *EEPA*). Oregon teens in four-day schools reported more food insecurity and drug use (Tomayko et al. 2021, *Journal of School Health*). That Oregon study used self-reported data and shows associations, not proven causes.
- What happens when the schedule spreads beyond rural areas.
- Whether a recruiting advantage lasts once neighboring districts adopt the schedule too.
- How much the way a week is designed (total instructional minutes) changes the results.
