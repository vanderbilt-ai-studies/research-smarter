> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for your 8-page argumentative public health paper, explored the topic, and chose the angle "exercise as a treatment for diabetes" because of your grandfather's experience with type 2 diabetes. You were at the last step of Phase 1, writing your draft research question, and now you've done that.

You've written it, and it's yours. It's also aimed at something that matters to real people, starting with your grandfather. Two things to work on before it can carry an argumentative paper:

**It can be answered with yes or no.** Your assignment asks you to argue a position, so the question needs room for thoughtful people to land in different places. A one-word answer leaves you nothing to argue for eight pages. **Since most people would expect the answer to be "yes," what about exercise and diabetes could people actually disagree about?**

**Some words are vague.** "Help" could mean lowering blood sugar, cutting medication, preventing complications, or something else. "Diabetes" covers more than one condition. **If a classmate read only your question, what would they think "help" means, and is that what you mean?**

Take either one first and revise the question yourself. We'll keep working on it until you're happy with it.
