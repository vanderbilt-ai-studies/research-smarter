## Second briefing: voucher effects on the students who use them

**Key debates**
- **Which outcomes count.** Several large studies from the 2010s found that test scores *fell* for voucher users. Supporters argue that longer-term outcomes like graduating and going to college matter more, and that those results look better. Critics say the test-score drops are real harm and shouldn't be explained away.
- **Why scores dropped.** The explanations are disputed. In Louisiana, the losses were concentrated in low-tuition private schools that chose to join the program. In DC, private schools reported about an hour less reading and math instruction per week. Others point to tests that line up with what public schools teach.
- **Short-term vs. long-term effects.** Some losses shrank over time, and others didn't.

**Major voices**
- Economists who ran lottery-based studies (Abdulkadiroğlu, Pathak, Walters).
- Researchers who have published many voucher studies and argue for school choice (Patrick Wolf, Paul Peterson).
- Researchers who read the same evidence as negative (Mark Dynarski, Austin Nichols).
- Federal evaluators: the Institute of Education Sciences (IES) evaluation of DC's voucher program.

**Patterns**
- **Recent statewide programs show early test-score losses,** mostly in math. This holds in Louisiana, Indiana, Ohio and DC. Louisiana's first-year math loss was large: 0.4 standard deviations.
- **College results look more mixed.** In Louisiana, voucher users entered college at about the same rate as the comparison group. In New York, some disadvantaged groups did better.
- **Who the studies looked at.** Most of them followed low-income students who switched out of public schools. Today's programs increasingly serve other students. In Indiana in 2025–26, more vouchers went to families earning over $100,000 than to families under it, and most voucher students had never attended a public school.

**Open questions**
- Do early losses turn into later gains?
- Do findings on low-income switchers apply to the much larger universal programs now running?
- What makes some private schools in these programs effective and others not?
