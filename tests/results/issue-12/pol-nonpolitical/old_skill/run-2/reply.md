Here are three more peer-reviewed sources on how bees adjust for the sun moving across the sky.

**Peer-reviewed article** (*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*)
Dyer FC, Dickinson JA. 1994. Development of sun compensation by honeybees: how partially experienced bees estimate the sun's course. Proc Natl Acad Sci USA. 91(10):4471–4474. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.91.10.4471
The researchers raised bees that had only ever seen the sun in the late afternoon. Then they watched how those bees aimed their waggle dances on a cloudy morning. The bees danced as if the morning sun sat roughly opposite the evening sun and stayed nearly still until about noon. The authors take this as evidence that bees are born with a general sense of how the sun moves. *Watch for:* it's an older study with a narrow, specially reared sample.

**Peer-reviewed article** (*Journal of Experimental Biology*)
Towne WF, Moscrip H. 2008. The connection between landscapes and the solar ephemeris in honeybees. J Exp Biol. 211(23):3729–3736. https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.022970
The researchers moved a hive to a "twin" landscape that faced a different direction, then tested the bees under overcast skies. The bees danced using a remembered link between the sun's daily path and the landscape around their home hive. That memory wasn't limited to routes they had already flown.

**Peer-reviewed article** (*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*)
Cheeseman JF, Millar CD, Greggers U, Lehmann K, Pawley MDM, Gallistel CR, Warman GR, Menzel R. 2014. Way-finding in displaced clock-shifted bees proves bees use a cognitive map. Proc Natl Acad Sci USA. 111(24):8949–8954. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1408039111
The researchers used anesthesia to shift the bees' internal clocks, which threw off their sun compass, and then moved the bees to a new spot. The shift didn't change how accurately or quickly the bees flew home once they noticed their first heading was wrong. The authors argue this rules out a purely sun-based homing explanation and supports a mental map. *Watch for:* PubMed lists two published comments responding to this paper, so read those too before you rely on it.
