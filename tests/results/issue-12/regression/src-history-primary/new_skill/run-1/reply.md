5. **Scholarly journal article.** Higgins, Jim. "An Epidemic's Strawman: Wilmer Krusen, Philadelphia's 1918–1919 Influenza Epidemic, and Historical Memory." *Pennsylvania Magazine of History and Biography* 144, no. 1 (2020): 61–88. DOI 10.1353/pmh.2020.0003

7. **Scholarly journal article.** Wilson, James M., Garrett M. Scalaro, and Jodie A. Powell. "Influenza Pandemic Warning Signals: Philadelphia in 1918 and 1977–1978." *Intelligence and National Security* 35, no. 4 (2020): 502–18. DOI 10.1080/02684527.2020.1750141
   Reviews the pattern of Philadelphia's local media reports in 1918. It's written from a public-health intelligence angle, not by historians, so its questions differ from yours.

8. **Scholarly journal article.** Dicke, Tom. "Waiting for the Flu: Cognitive Inertia and the Spanish Influenza Pandemic of 1918–19." *Journal of the History of Medicine and Allied Sciences* 70, no. 2 (2015): 195–217. https://doi.org/10.1093/jhmas/jru019
   Studies US newspaper coverage from June to September 1918 and finds little alarm or preparation in places the flu hadn't reached yet. It explains this mainly as "cognitive inertia." The main case study is Springfield, Missouri, not Philadelphia.

9. **Scholarly journal article.** Hume, Janice. "The 'Forgotten' 1918 Influenza Epidemic and Press Portrayal of Public Anxiety." *Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly* 77, no. 4 (2000): 898–915. DOI 10.1177/107769900007700411
   Looks for signs of public anxiety in coverage of the epidemic and asks why it was forgotten. According to the abstract, it studies national **magazines**, not daily newspapers.

10. **Scholarly journal article.** Honigsbaum, Mark. "Regulating the 1918–19 Pandemic: Flu, Stoicism and the Northcliffe Press." *Medical History* 57, no. 2 (2013): 165–85. https://doi.org/10.1017/mdh.2012.101 (free full text on [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3867839))
