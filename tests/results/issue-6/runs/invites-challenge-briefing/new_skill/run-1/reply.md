## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Do they save real money?** A 2021 RAND brief says that "more than any other factor, the desire to save money" drives districts to switch. It also reports that measured savings are usually under 5%, while people often assume savings of up to 20%. The reason is that salaries and benefits, the biggest costs, stay the same with a shorter week. Other estimates put savings at about 0.4–2.5%.
- **Is money really the main motive?** Sources disagree. Paul Thompson's 24-state study says many rural districts switch for practical reasons like long bus rides, where cutting a day saves little. In Missouri, districts most often named teacher hiring and retention as their main reason.
- **Does learning suffer?** Recent multi-state work finds losses on average. They're small or close to zero in rural districts, but meaningful in town and suburban ones.

**Major voices**
- Economist Paul Thompson (Oregon State) and Emily Morton (NWEA) wrote many of the main studies.
- RAND ran a large study in Idaho, New Mexico, and Oklahoma.
- CALDER published working papers on teachers in Missouri and Colorado.
- The University of Oregon's HEDCO Institute reviewed 11 causal studies and found "no evidence of large positive effects."

**Patterns**
- The schedule has spread fast, from 257 schools in 1999 to more than 2,100 in about 26 states. About 90% are rural.
- Losses in learning seem to follow lost hours more than the lost day itself.
- District claims often don't match the data. For example, Independence, Missouri reported a big jump in job applications, but a 2025 CALDER study found no recruitment or retention gains across Missouri.
- Families often like the schedule. In RAND's survey, 84% of elementary parents said they'd choose four days.

**Open questions**
- Effects on child care, school meals, and family costs on the off day haven't been studied much.
- Behavior evidence points in different directions. Morton found fewer fights and less bullying in Oklahoma schools, while Fischer and Argyle found more juvenile crime in Colorado areas with four-day weeks.
- Few studies compare why districts *say* they switched with what actually changed afterward.
