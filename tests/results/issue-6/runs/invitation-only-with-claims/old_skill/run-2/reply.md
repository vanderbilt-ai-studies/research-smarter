Here's your position as you wrote it: four-day weeks are better for teachers than for students, because teachers get a day to plan and rest but kids lose class time. That's a real thesis. It takes a side, and comparing two groups gives your research question a clear shape.

**The "kids lose class time" part is only partly accurate.** Most four-day districts make the remaining days longer, by about 52 minutes on average. So the real question is how much total time students lose, and that varies a lot from district to district. A 2022 study found no significant difference in achievement between four-day and five-day districts when the four-day districts kept a high or medium amount of instructional time. The biggest drops in math and reading showed up in districts that cut the most time. A larger 2024 study across six states (Morton, Thompson, and Kuhfeld) still found that students learned less on average on four-day schedules. So both of these hold: students often lose some learning, and it depends on how the district sets up its schedule.
