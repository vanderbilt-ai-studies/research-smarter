**On "kids lose class time":** the evidence mostly backs you, with one catch.
- RAND's large 2021 study, *Does Four Equal Five?*, found that four-day districts have longer days but fewer instructional hours across the year. Students' test scores still grew, but more slowly than their five-day peers' ([RAND brief](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_briefs/RBA300/RBA373-1/RAND_RBA373-1.pdf), [NWEA summary](https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/does-four-equal-five-implementation-and-outcomes-of-the-four-day-school-week/)).
- The catch is that it varies by district. A related paper found achievement dropped in districts that cut a lot of school time. In districts that kept time in school at middle or high levels, the effects weren't statistically significant ([KGOU coverage](https://www.kgou.org/education/2021-10-13/massive-four-day-school-week-study-shows-effects-of-the-practice-are-mixed)).

So "kids lose class time" is true in many districts but not all. How might that affect your thesis, and your cousin's district in particular?
