**What's already strong:**
- **It fits your method.** A "what is it like" question asks about experience, and that's what qualitative interviews are good at.
- **It's open and neutral.** It can't be answered yes or no, and it doesn't assume that asking for help is hard. That leaves room for interviewees to surprise you.
- **It matters.** You can already say who it matters to and why, and you have an insider's view of it.

**Where I'd push:** the phrase "what is it like" covers a lot. It could mean how students feel, what holds them back, how they decide whether to go, what happens when they do go, or how they learn the unwritten rules. Each of those would lead to a different interview guide.

Imagine you're sitting across from your first interviewee. What would you most want them to tell you about?

Once you've thought about that, decide whether your question needs to change to point there. If it does, rewrite it in your own words. If not, explain why the current wording already captures it.
