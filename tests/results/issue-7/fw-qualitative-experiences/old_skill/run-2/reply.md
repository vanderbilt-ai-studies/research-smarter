Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for your 8-page sociology proposal for a qualitative interview study, and you chose the angle of asking professors for help. You picked it because you're first-gen and it took you a whole semester to get to office hours. The step left was writing your draft research question, and you've now done that.

Your draft has real strengths. It can't be answered with yes or no. It doesn't assume what students will say. And "what is it like" suits interviews, because it asks for people's experiences rather than numbers.

The part I'd look at is the core phrase, "what is it like." Imagine you're 40 minutes into an interview and the student has told you a lot. You need to know what you're listening for.

If a classmate read only your question, which part of the experience would they think you most want to understand?
