> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for your 8-page sociology proposal, an interview study due May 20, 2027. You picked "asking professors for help" because it took you a whole semester to get yourself to office hours. You're at the last step of Phase 1, writing your draft question, and you've just done it.

Here's what's strong about it. It's open: nobody can answer it with yes or no. It's neutral, since it doesn't assume asking for help is hard, even though your own experience might suggest that. And "asking professors for help" is a specific behavior, which suits interviews. You'll be hearing about something your participants have actually done or avoided.

Two parts are still loose for a qualitative proposal:

- **"What is it like."** If a classmate read only your question, what do you think they'd expect your interviews to uncover? It could be feelings, the reasons students hold back, how they finally decide to go, or something else.
- **Which students.** First-gen students at a community college, a big public university and a small private college could have very different experiences of reaching out. Where are you picturing the people you'd interview?

Take either one, or both, and revise the question in your own words when you're ready.
