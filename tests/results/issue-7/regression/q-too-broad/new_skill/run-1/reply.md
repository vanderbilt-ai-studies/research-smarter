Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for your 8-page psychology literature review and chose the angle "sleep and how well people think," because you're always tired during exams. The last step left in this phase is your draft question, and you've written one.

Your draft is clear, neutral, and can't be answered with yes or no, so it already meets three of the criteria for a good question. Its weak spot is scope. "Sleep" and "human cognition" each cover a whole field. A review question about an exposure and an outcome usually names three parts: the **population** (who), the **exposure** (which part of sleep, such as how long, how well, or when), and the **outcome** (which kind of thinking). Right now your question leaves all three open.

You could answer it in eight pages, but you'd have to leave a lot out. What would you leave out?
