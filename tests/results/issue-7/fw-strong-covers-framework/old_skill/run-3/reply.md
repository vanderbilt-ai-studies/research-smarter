Welcome back. Last time you finished intake for your 12-page nursing research proposal, explored the landscape, and chose nurse-led walking programs for diabetes in rural clinics, because you want to work in a rural clinic after you graduate. That left one step in Phase 1: writing your draft research question. You've now written it.

This is a strong draft. It names the population (adults with type 2 diabetes), the setting (rural clinics), the intervention (a nurse-led walking program), a fair comparison (standard written advice), a measurable outcome, and a timeframe. Someone reading it would know exactly what a study would test. It also doesn't assume its own answer: it asks whether the program works better, not why it does. For a quantitative proposal built on a hypothesis, a directional comparison like this is a good fit, and the rural setting is your own contribution, which ties back to why you chose the topic.

The part I'd look at more closely is the outcome. A proposal reviewer will want to know why you chose that particular measure for a six-month study.

**What made you pick fasting blood glucose as the outcome, and what do you know about how well a single fasting reading captures someone's glucose control over six months?**

Once you've thought that through, you decide whether the question stays as it is or changes. After that you'll be ready to move on to Phase 2, where we stress-test it.
