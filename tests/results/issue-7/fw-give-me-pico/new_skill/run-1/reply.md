Welcome back. You're working on a 10-page health sciences literature review, due May 20, 2027. You chose the angle of teenagers' screen time before bed, partly because you're often on your phone until 2am yourself. The step you stopped at is writing your draft research question.

I won't write the PICO question for you. It's the backbone of your whole review, so it needs to be yours. What I can do is make it easier by breaking it into four small parts you fill in one at a time:

- **P (Population):** who exactly you're asking about
- **I (Intervention):** the treatment or change you're looking at
- **C (Comparison):** what you're comparing it against
- **O (Outcome):** what you'd measure to see an effect

One thing to watch for: PICO is built for questions about whether an intervention works. Screen time before bed is something teens already do, not a treatment. So part of building your question is deciding what will count as the "I." That's a real choice, and it will shape which studies end up in your review.

Let's start with the first part. Who is your Population, and why that group?
