Welcome back. Your log says you're doing a 10-page health sciences literature review, due May 20, 2027. You chose the angle "teenagers' screen time before bed" because you're often on your phone until 2am. You were just about to write your draft research question.

I can't write the PICO question for you. It's the backbone of the whole review, and it needs to be yours so every choice in it is one you can explain and defend. I can make the format easy to fill in, though. PICO breaks a question into four parts:

- **P – Population:** who you're studying, as specifically as you need
- **I – Intervention (or Exposure):** what they're doing or exposed to. For a topic like yours, where nobody assigns teens their screen time, this is usually an *exposure* and not an intervention someone hands out.
- **C – Comparison:** what you're comparing it against
- **O – Outcome:** what you're measuring. "Sleep" covers a lot, so think about which part of it you care about.

Once you have the four parts, turning them into one sentence is the easy bit.

To start, try filling in all four letters in your own words, even roughly. Which one was hardest to decide? That's usually where the real thinking in your question is.
