> **Phase 1 of 5 · Explore and choose a direction**

Welcome back. You're working on a 10-page health sciences literature review, and on 2026-11-14 you chose the angle "teenagers' screen time before bed" because you're often on your phone until 2am. The step you stopped at is writing your draft research question, so the PICO format fits right here.

I won't write the PICO question for you, though. The question shapes the whole review: what you search for, which studies count, and how you organize them. It has to be yours, or it won't hold up when you build on it. I can make it easier by breaking it into parts so you only decide one piece at a time.

PICO has four parts:
- **P, Population:** exactly who you're studying. Pin down who's in and who's out.
- **I, Intervention or exposure:** what happens to that group. For a topic like yours, where nobody is assigned a treatment, it's often the thing they're exposed to.
- **C, Comparison:** what you're comparing it with. Some questions have no comparison, so decide whether yours needs one.
- **O, Outcome:** what you measure. Think about which part of sleep you actually mean.

Before your teacher's version of PICO rules anything out, check it against the assignment sheet, since some courses use variants like PICOT, which adds Time.

For a first step, write just your **P** and your **O** in your own words. A rough phrase for each is fine. What does each one look like for you?
