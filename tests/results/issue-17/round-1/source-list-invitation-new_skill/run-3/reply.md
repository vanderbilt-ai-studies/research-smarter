## What I found and didn't find

- **Plenty of data:** wellbeing, phone use, and bullying measured as a whole.
- **Thin:** in-person interaction. It comes up only in what students and staff *say* in interviews and focus groups (sources 5, 8, 11). None of the studies I could read measured it directly, for example by observing or counting conversations.
- **Missing:** any study that follows conflict *moving to after school*. The closest is source 11, which describes conflict moving in the *other* direction, from online after school into the school day. Bullying measures also usually don't separate in-school from out-of-school bullying.
- **Age:** a lot of the strongest data is on 12–15-year-olds or middle schools (sources 2, 3, 4). Sources 6–8 are the closest to high school age.

## Open issues the results raise

- The Spain and Norway studies count bullying overall, so a drop there could mean less conflict, or conflict that just isn't being reported.
- Some studies find students make up their lost phone time later in the day. That could also be when online conflict happens.
- A ban could increase in-person interaction for some students and make it harder for others (source 8).
