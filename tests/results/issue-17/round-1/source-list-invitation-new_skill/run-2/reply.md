## Sources

**Peer-reviewed studies (original research)**

1. **Peer-reviewed article.** Goodyear, V. A., Randhawa, A., Adab, P., Al-Janabi, H., Fenton, S., Jones, K., Michail, M., Morrison, B., Patterson, P., Quinlan, J., Sitch, A., Twardochleb, R., Wade, M., & Pallan, M. (2025). School phone policies and their association with mental wellbeing, phone use, and social media use (SMART Schools): A cross-sectional observational study. *The Lancet Regional Health – Europe, 51*, 101211. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2025.101211
   The study compared 1,227 students aged 12–15 in 30 English schools: 20 with restrictive phone policies and 10 with permissive ones. Students in restrictive schools used their phones less during school, but total phone and social media time over weekdays and weekends was no different, and neither was wellbeing.

2. **Peer-reviewed article.** Goodyear, V. A., Randhawa, A., Adab, P., Al-Janabi, H., Fenton, S., Michail, M., Patterson, P., Sitch, A., Wade, M., & Pallan, M. (2026). How school phone policies influence adolescent phone use and wellbeing (SMART Schools): A qualitative comparative case study. *Social Science & Medicine, 398*, 119094. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2026.119094
   This is the qualitative follow-up: 40 focus groups with 177 students, parents and staff in 7 English schools. The abstract says both kinds of policy had positive and negative effects. The specific points about more face-to-face interaction in restrictive schools and conflicts from social media spilling into school come from the university's press release, not the paper. I couldn't open the full text.

3. **Peer-reviewed article.** Vanluydt, E., van den Eijnden, R., Vonk, L., Putrik, P., van Amelsvoort, T., Delespaul, P., Levels, M., & Huijts, T. (2026). Disconnect to reconnect: How variations between types of smartphone bans influence students' well-being and social connectedness in Dutch secondary education. *Journal of Youth and Adolescence, 55*(3), 551–569. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-025-02313-6
   The study compared 1,398 Dutch students (average age 16.2) in schools with full bans and schools with classroom-only bans. It found no differences in wellbeing or bullying. Full bans were linked to weaker student–teacher connectedness and, for girls, a weaker sense of belonging at school.

4. **Peer-reviewed article.** Vereecke, J., Vanherle, R., Nassen, L.-M., Boen, H., Eggermont, S., Beullens, K., Vandenbosch, L., & Schreurs, L. (2026). More than disconnecting? School smartphone bans and adolescents' social wellbeing. *Frontiers in Developmental Psychology, 4*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fdpys.2026.1796471
   This followed 960 students at one Flemish (Belgian) school for a year after it switched to a total ban. Social loneliness at school rose slightly over the year and friend support stayed flat. Cyberbullying perpetration dipped slightly at first. Bullying was measured only online, there was no comparison school, and phone use outside school wasn't measured.

5. **Peer-reviewed article.** King, D. L., Radunz, M., Galanis, C. R., Quinney, B., & Wade, T. (2024). "Phones off while school's on": Evaluating problematic phone use and the social, wellbeing, and academic effects of banning phones in schools. *Journal of Behavioral Addictions, 13*(4), 913–922. https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2024.00058
   This preregistered study surveyed about 1,280 students in Grades 7–12 at five South Australian schools before a ban and one month after. Schools with and without the ban didn't differ on belonging or engagement. Bullying was slightly higher in the ban group but fell in both groups.

6. **Peer-reviewed article.** Bar, E., Radunz, M., Galanis, C. R., Quinney, B., Wade, T. D., & King, D. L. (2025). Student perspectives on banning mobile phones in South Australian secondary schools: A large-scale qualitative analysis. *Computers in Human Behavior, 167*, 108603. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108603
   The same research team analyzed 7,188 open-ended answers from 1,549 students. Students named face-to-face social interaction as a benefit of the ban. They also described downsides, such as finding it harder to manage emotional distress without their phones.

7. **Peer-reviewed article.** Abrahamsson, S. (2026). Smartphone bans, student outcomes and mental health. *Journal of Human Resources*. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.0224-13403R2
   This uses administrative and survey data to study bans in middle schools. The paper is paywalled, so I read only the abstract. It reports less bullying for both boys and girls, plus academic and mental-health-care gains for girls. The abstract doesn't name the country; news coverage I saw in search results says Norway.

8. **Peer-reviewed article.** Beneito, P., & Vicente-Chirivella, Ó. (2022). Banning mobile phones in schools: Evidence from regional-level policies in Spain. *Applied Economic Analysis, 30*(90), 153–175. https://doi.org/10.1108/aea-05-2021-0112
   This compared two Spanish regions that banned phones in 2015 with the rest of Spain, using region-level data. The abstract reports "noticeable reductions of bullying incidence" among teenagers in the regions with bans.

**Peer-reviewed reviews**

9. **Peer-reviewed article (scoping review).** Campbell, M., Edwards, E. J., Pennell, D., Poed, S., Lister, V., Gillett-Swan, J., Kelly, A., Zec, D., & Nguyen, T.-A. (2024). Evidence for and against banning mobile phones in schools: A scoping review. *Journal of Psychologists and Counsellors in Schools, 34*(3), 242–265. https://doi.org/10.1177/20556365241270394
   The review covers 22 studies on academic outcomes, wellbeing and cyberbullying. It found no randomized trials and studies too varied in design to reconcile easily. It's a good map of earlier work.

10. **Peer-reviewed article (rapid review and meta-analysis).** Böttger, T., & Zierer, K. (2024). To ban or not to ban? A rapid review on the impact of smartphone bans in schools on social well-being and academic performance. *Education Sciences, 14*(8), 906. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14080906
    This pooled five quantitative studies and found a small overall effect, larger for social outcomes like bullying than for grades. Five studies is a thin base for a meta-analysis.

**Research organization report**

11. **Research organization report.** Reynolds, M., Esfandiari, M., & O'Higgins Norman, J. (2025). *Smartphone bans in schools: A qualitative study of the experiences of students*. DCU Anti-Bullying Centre, Dublin City University. https://www.dcu.ie/antibullyingcentre/restriction-or-resilience
    This is a set of focus groups with 66 Irish students aged 10–18. It's not peer reviewed and the sample is small, so it's useful for what students say, not for measuring effects. In the report's words, students "clearly stated that the bans just move any cyberbullying behaviour until after school" and "indicated that the ban does not promote socialising." It also found that "the stricter the phone ban the more students look for ways to subvert it."

## What I found and didn't find

- **Plentiful:** studies on bullying and wellbeing, and students' own reports of talking more face to face.
- **Thin:** direct measures of in-person interaction. Nearly all the evidence on face-to-face talk comes from what students say in surveys or focus groups. I found no study that observed or counted conversations.
- **Missing:** I found no study that tracks conflict before and after school hours separately, and no US high school studies. Several samples are younger than high school (Goodyear: ages 12–15; Abrahamsson: middle school).
- **Couldn't read in full:** the full texts of 2, 3, 6, 7 and 10. For those I worked from the abstracts or, for source 2, a press release.

## Questions the results raise

- Most studies measure cyberbullying or "bullying" in general. What does "conflict" mean in your position, and which of these measures would actually capture it moving to after school?
- Sources 3 and 4 measure connectedness and belonging rather than how much students talk. Is more talk the same thing as better relationships for your argument?
