## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Do test scores suffer?** This is the main fight. In Colorado, Anderson and Walker (*Education Finance and Policy*, 2015) found no harm and even some positive links. In Oregon, Thompson (*Journal of Public Economics*, 2021) found small drops in both math and reading. In the working-paper version, math fell 0.044–0.053 standard deviations and reading 0.033–0.038 ([IZA paper](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/12204)).
- **Is a small academic cost worth the other benefits?** RAND's 2021 multi-state study found families were very satisfied with the schedule. It also found scores grew more slowly than they would have under a five-day week, and cost savings were small. I opened the RAND brief but couldn't read its text, so this comes from search summaries.

**Major voices**
- Economist Paul Thompson (Oregon State) is behind much of the recent research.
- RAND and NWEA researchers, including Emily Morton, lead the multi-state work.
- [The Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/education/four-day-school-week-research/) (Harvard's Shorenstein Center) keeps a good overview of the studies.

**Patterns**
- **How much time schools keep seems to matter.** Schools that keep total hours close to a five-day week often show little change, while schools that cut hours show larger declines.
- **Gaps may grow over time.** In Oregon, Thompson reported drops of about 9–10% of a standard deviation four years after a switch ([Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/shrinking-school-week-effects-four-day-schedule-student-achievement/)).
- **Adoption is growing fast.** Thompson estimates more than 2,100 schools in 26 states now use the schedule. In Texas, schools using it went from 30 in 2020–21 to 506 in 2024–25 (via Journalist's Resource, citing the *Texas Tribune*).

**Open questions**
- **Effects outside academics are thin and point different ways.** A Colorado study (Fischer & Argyle, 2018) linked the schedule to about 20% more juvenile crime. An Oklahoma study (Morton, 2022) found less fighting and bullying in school. I've seen both only secondhand, through the Journalist's Resource overview.
- **Some areas are barely studied:**
  - teacher retention
  - childcare and meals on the off day
  - effects on rural communities, where most four-day schools are
- **Effects differ by student.** An Oregon early-elementary study ([Thompson et al., *Early Childhood Research Quarterly*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9979633)) found the strongest students were hurt most, while some other groups showed no significant harm.
