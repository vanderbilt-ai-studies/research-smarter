## Preliminary briefing

**Key debates**
- **Learning:** Does a four-day week hurt learning? A University of Oregon review of 11 studies found "little evidence" of benefits for achievement, attendance, behavior, or graduation ([UO News, 2025](https://news.uoregon.edu/node/13412)). One argument is that the schedule itself matters less than how many hours it cuts.
- **Money:** Is it worth it financially? Reported savings are about 0.4% to 2.5% of a district's budget ([Education Week, 2026](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/the-4-day-school-week-what-research-shows-about-the-alternative-schedule/2026/04)).
- **Teachers:** Does it help with hiring and keeping teachers? A Texas study found teacher turnover fell. A Denver-area district found retention went down ([EdWeek, 2026](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/the-4-day-school-week-what-research-shows-about-the-alternative-schedule/2026/04); [EdWeek, 2023](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/four-studies-to-know-on-four-day-school-weeks/2023/02)).
- **The day off:** What happens on the fifth day? A Colorado study linked the schedule to about 20% more youth crime, mostly property crime ([Chalkbeat, 2018](https://chalkbeat.org/2018/4/2/21104689/four-day-school-weeks-a-nationwide-symptom-of-tight-budgets-lead-to-more-youth-crime-study-finds)). Oklahoma research found less bullying and fewer fights at school.

**Major voices**
- **Paul Thompson** (Oregon State) and **Emily Morton** (NWEA) wrote much of the recent research.
- The **HEDCO Institute** at the University of Oregon did the systematic review.
- **RAND** published a multi-state study in 2021.
- Economists **Stefanie Fischer and Daniel Argyle** did the crime study.

**Patterns**
- Most studies compare districts that switched with districts that didn't, using state data from a few states. Oregon, Oklahoma, and Colorado come up most.
- Results often differ between rural and non-rural districts and between age groups. In one early-elementary study, average differences were "minimal." The students hurt most were those already doing well, not disadvantaged groups ([Thompson et al., 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9979633)).
- Adoption keeps growing despite the mixed evidence. More than 2,100 schools in 26 states used the schedule in 2024–25.

**Open questions**
- Teacher recruitment, family childcare, and effects on rural communities have little research behind them.
- Little is known about how districts actually decide to switch.
- A 2026 study revisits the crime question ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/education/four-day-school-week-research/) is a good guide to what else exists).
