2. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Journal of Youth and Adolescence*). Vanluydt, E., van den Eijnden, R., Vonk, L., Putrik, P., van Amelsvoort, T., Delespaul, P., Levels, M., & Huijts, T. (2026). Disconnect to reconnect: How variations between types of smartphone bans influence students' well-being and social connectedness in Dutch secondary education. *Journal of Youth and Adolescence, 55*(3), 551–569. https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/disconnect-to-reconnect-how-variations-between-types-of-smartphon/

3. **Peer-reviewed article** (*Journal of Behavioral Addictions*). King, D. L., Radunz, M., Galanis, C. R., Quinney, B., & Wade, T. (2024). ## What I found and didn't find

- **Bullying:** There's a fair amount of evidence, and it points in different directions across countries and study designs.
- **Face-to-face interaction:** The evidence is thin. I found only qualitative accounts of students' views (#6, #8). I didn't find any study that observed or counted in-person interaction.
- **Conflict moving after school:** I found no study that measures this directly. The closest are data on phone use outside school (#1, #9). That's about use, not conflict.
- **US high schools specifically:** Very little. Most studies are from Europe and Australia, and several cover younger students.

## Questions the results raise

- Several studies define a "ban" differently (classroom only vs. whole school grounds). That may matter for what your position claims.
- "Social connectedness" and "face-to-face interaction" aren't the same thing. Sources measure different versions of what you're calling interaction.
