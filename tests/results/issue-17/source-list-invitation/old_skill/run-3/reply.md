3. **Peer-reviewed article.** Vanluydt, E., van den Eijnden, R., Vonk, L., Putrik, P., van Amelsvoort, T., Delespaul, P., Levels, M., & Huijts, T. (2026). Disconnect to reconnect: How variations between types of smartphone bans influence students' well-being and social connectedness in Dutch secondary education. *Journal of Youth and Adolescence, 55*(3), 551–569. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-025-02313-6 (the DOI led to a login wall). Open author copy: https://publications.tno.nl/publication/34645498/0SaTpWan/vanluydt-2026-disconnect.pdf
   The study compared classroom-only bans with whole-school bans in 24 Dutch schools (1,398 students, average age 16.2). The researchers found no differences in wellbeing or bullying. Whole-school bans were linked to weaker student–teacher connectedness and, for girls, a lower sense of belonging at school.

4. **Peer-reviewed article.** King, D. L., Radunz, M., Galanis, C. R., Quinney, B., & Wade, T. (2024). "Phones off while school's on": Evaluating problematic phone use and the social, wellbeing, and academic effects of banning phones in schools. *Journal of Behavioral Addictions, 13*(4), 913–922. https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2024.00058 (the publisher page blocked me). Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11737419/
   This pre-registered before-and-after comparison followed South Australian schools that did and didn't adopt a ban. It found no significant differences in school belonging or academic engagement. Bullying fell in both groups, and it was slightly higher in the ban group.

5. **Peer-reviewed article.** Beneito, P., & Vicente-Chirivella, Ó. (2022). Banning mobile phones in schools: Evidence from regional-level policies in Spain. *Applied Economic Analysis, 30*(90), 153–175. https://doi.org/10.1108/AEA-05-2021-0112
   The authors compared two Spanish regions that banned phones around 2015 with regions that didn't. Officially reported bullying among teenagers fell by roughly 9.5% to 18% in the regions with bans. The data don't separate online bullying from in-person bullying.

6. **Peer-reviewed article.** Abrahamsson, S. (2026). Smartphone bans, student outcomes and mental health. *Journal of Human Resources.* Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.0224-13403R2
   The study used Norwegian administrative and survey data on middle schools. It found no average effect on education or mental health, but bullying went down for both boys and girls after bans. I could read only the abstract, which doesn't say whether the bullying was online or in person.

7. **Peer-reviewed article (scoping review).** Campbell, M., Edwards, E., Pennell, D., Poed, S., Lister, V., Gillett-Swan, J., Kelly, A., Zec, D., & Nguyen, T.-A. (2024). Evidence for and against banning mobile phones in schools: A scoping review. *Journal of Psychologists and Counsellors in Schools, 34*(3), 242–265. https://doi.org/10.1177/20556365241270394 (the publisher page blocked me). Repository record: https://eprints.qut.edu.au/251231/
   The review covers 22 studies on academic outcomes, wellbeing and cyberbullying. It found no randomized trials and a small number of studies that defined "ban" in different ways. I read only the abstract.

## What I found and didn't find

- **Plentiful:** studies of wellbeing, screen time and test scores.
- **Thin:** direct measures of face-to-face talk. The evidence is mostly qualitative (sources 2 and 8) or uses stand-ins like "belonging" and "connectedness" (sources 3 and 4).
- **Not found:** any study that tracks whether cyberbullying moves to after school. Most bullying measures I found don't separate online from in-person bullying. Source 1 shows that phone *use* shifts outside school, but that is about screen time, not conflict.
- **Age mismatch:** several samples are middle schoolers or 12–15-year-olds. Source 3 is the closest to high school age. I found no peer-reviewed study of US high schools.

## Questions the results raise

- Whether a "belonging" or "connectedness" score captures the kind of in-person interaction your question is about.
- What it means for your claim about conflict "moving" if bullying data can't tell online and in-person bullying apart.
- How much results from Norway, Spain, England, the Netherlands and Australia can tell you about US high schools.
