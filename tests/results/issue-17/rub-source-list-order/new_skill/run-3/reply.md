**Peer-reviewed articles** (these three meet your 3-peer-reviewed minimum)

- **Peer-reviewed article** (*PNAS*). Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122*(26), e2422633122. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
  This was a field experiment with nearly 1,000 high school math students in Turkey. While students had access, a standard ChatGPT-style tutor raised practice grades by 48%. Once access was removed, those students scored 17% lower than students who never had it. A tutor built to give hints instead of answers largely avoided that drop. *I read the abstract only, because the publisher page blocked me. A correction was published in August 2025 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2518204122), and I couldn't see what it changed.*

- **Peer-reviewed article** (*British Journal of Educational Technology*). Fan, Y., Tang, L., Le, H., Shen, K., Tan, S., Zhao, Y., Shen, Y., Li, X., & Gašević, D. (2025). Beware of metacognitive laziness: Effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning motivation, processes, and performance. *British Journal of Educational Technology, 56*(2), 489–530. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13544
  This was a randomized experiment with 117 university students on a writing task. They got help from ChatGPT, a human expert, writing tools, or nothing. The ChatGPT group's essay scores improved more, but knowledge gain and transfer were no different, and that group did fewer self-monitoring steps such as evaluating their own work.

- **Peer-reviewed article** (*Computers & Education*). Deng, R., Jiang, M., Yu, X., Lu, Y., & Liu, S. (2025). Does ChatGPT enhance student learning? A systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental studies. *Computers & Education, 227*, 105224. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105224
  This review covers 69 experimental studies from 2022 to 2024, mostly at the university level. It finds that ChatGPT improves academic performance and reduces mental effort. It also warns that many studies were small, and that the tests may have measured the quality of the AI's output rather than what students learned.

**Preprint and working papers** (not peer reviewed)

- **Preprint, not yet peer reviewed** (arXiv). Lehmann, M., Cornelius, P. B., & Sting, F. J. (2025). *AI meets the classroom: When do large language models harm learning?* (arXiv:2409.09047v2). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047
  It reports two lab experiments and a field study of coding students, and found no overall effect on learning. Students who had the AI do the work covered more topics but understood each one less. Students who asked it for explanations understood more. *It's here because it compares kinds of use directly. Watch out: it's university students, it isn't peer reviewed yet, and version 1 had a different title and framing.*

- **Working paper (research organization)**, CEPR Discussion Paper. Strömberg, D., Lei, V., & Wu, Y. (2026). *The generative AI learning penalty: Evidence from Chinese secondary education* (Discussion Paper No. 21577). Centre for Economic Policy Research. https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/21577.html
  It follows 26,811 students in grades 7 to 12 over 30 months. After adopting AI, homework scores rose 18% and monthly exam scores fell 20%. The losses were concentrated in the roughly 80% of users whose homework times suggest outsourcing. *It's here because it's large, recent, and about secondary students. Watch out: it's not peer reviewed, and AI use and "outsourcing" are inferred from homework data, not observed. I read only the abstract, on the IDEAS listing.*

- **Working paper (international agency)**, World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11125. De Simone, M., Tiberti, F., Barron Rodriguez, M., Manolio, F., Mosuro, W., & Dikoru, E. J.   This randomized trial gave senior secondary students in Nigeria six weeks of after-school English tutoring with GPT-4 (through Microsoft Copilot), with teachers guiding the sessions. English scores rose by 0.23 standard deviations. *It's here because it's a rigorous high school trial. Watch out: it's a guided program, not unsupervised homework, and it isn't peer reviewed.
