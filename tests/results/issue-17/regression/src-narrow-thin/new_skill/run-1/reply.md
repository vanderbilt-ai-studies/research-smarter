## Sources

**Peer-reviewed articles.** All four meet your requirement of two peer-reviewed sources. ERIC, the U.S. education research database, marks each one "Peer reviewed."

1. **Peer-reviewed article.** Morton, E. (2023). Effects of 4-day school weeks on older adolescents: Examining impacts of the schedule on academic achievement, attendance, and behavior in high school. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 45*(1), 52–78. https://doi.org/10.3102/01623737221097420 ([ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1365320))
   Uses Oklahoma district data to compare high schools before and after they switched to four days. It found about 39% less bullying and 31% less fighting, and no detectable change in ACT scores or attendance. An earlier listing gives the year as 2022, but the journal issue is 2023, so cite 2023.

2. **Peer-reviewed article.** Thompson, P. N., Gunter, K., Schuna, J. M., Jr., & Tomayko, E. J. (2021). Are all four-day school weeks created equal? A national assessment of four-day school week policy adoption and implementation. *Education Finance and Policy, 16*(4), 558–583. https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00316 ([ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1316838))
   A national dataset of four-day schools, built from school calendars. Districts mostly adopt the schedule to save money, and most are small and rural. The day off is often Friday, and four-day schools average 148 school days a year compared with about 180.

3. **Peer-reviewed article.** Turner, J., Finch, K., & Uribe-Zarain, X. (2019). Three Midwest rural school districts' first year transition to the four day school week: Parents' perspectives. *The Rural Educator, 40*(1), 1–15. https://doi.org/10.35608/ruraled.v40i1.529
   Surveys 584 parents in three rural Missouri districts during their first year on four days. Support was strong in every group, but lower among families with only elementary-age children or children in special education. I read only the abstract, so I don't know whether the full text discusses activities.

4. **Peer-reviewed article.** Hewitt, P. M., & Denny, G. S. (2011). The four-day school week: Impact on student academic performance. *The Rural Educator, 32*(2), 23–31. [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ987605)
   Compares Colorado test scores in grades 3–10 between four-day and five-day schools. Five-day schools scored higher in 11 of 12 comparisons, but only one difference was statistically significant. This is background on four-day weeks; it doesn't address activities.

**Research organization reports**

5. **Research organization report.** Kilburn, M. R., Phillips, A., Gomez, C. J., Mariano, L. T., Doss, C. J., Troxel, W. M., Morton, E., & Estes, K. (2021). *Does four equal five? Implementation and outcomes of the four-day school week* (RR-A373-1). RAND Corporation. https://doi.org/10.7249/RR-A373-1 ([report page](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA373-1.html))
   A 202-page study of districts in Idaho, New Mexico, and Oklahoma, funded by the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation. It found small cost savings, high satisfaction, lower reading and math scores over time, and that students in grades 7–12 reported about 3.5 more hours of free time a week. *Why it's here:* it's the largest study of how students use the extra time. News coverage says it covers sports, but I could only read the summary page, not the full report.

6. **Research organization report.** HEDCO Institute for Evidence-Based Educational Practice. (2025). *Four-day school week scoping review*. University of Oregon. [Review page](https://hedcoinstitute.uoregon.edu/reports/four-day-school-week-scoping-review)
   A review that is updated over time (last in June 2025) and tries to list every U.S. study of four-day weeks: 111 studies, plus interviews with three superintendents. *Why it's here:* it's a map for finding more studies, not evidence by itself.

**Doctoral dissertations.** These are university-reviewed but not peer-reviewed. Use them with care.

7. **Doctoral dissertation.** Morton, E. (2021). *Can four equal five? Essays on the four-day school week* [Doctoral dissertation, Stanford University]. https://purl.stanford.edu/rz289sr3469
   Reports that four-day students spend the day off "primarily at home with adult supervision." It also says they spend more time each week on "chores, non-school sports or exercise, hobbies, jobs, and homework." *Why it's here:* it has time-use data the peer-reviewed article doesn't. Where its findings overlap with Morton (2023), cite the published article instead.

8. **Doctoral dissertation.** Holmes, W. E. (2025). *Improving the work-life balance of the high school marching band director: A case study* [Doctoral dissertation, Liberty University]. https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/doctoral/6700
   A case study of one high school band that went from four rehearsals a week to two. It found no significant change in competition scores, plus more time for student tutoring and a better work-life balance for the director. *Why it's here:* it's the only study I found that measures what fewer rehearsals do to a marching band. But it covers one band, and the cut was the band's own choice, not a four-day school week.

**Primary source**

9. **Primary source (official rule).** University Interscholastic League. (n.d.). *Eight hour marching band rule FAQ*. https://www.uiltexas.org/music/marching-band/eight-hour-marching-band-rule-qa
   Texas's governing body for school competitions caps marching band practice outside school at 8 hours a week. In a week with fewer than five school days, a band "may rehearse one additional hour beyond 8 hours for each non-school day." *Why it's here:* it's a real rule that shapes rehearsal time in at least one state. Treat it as material to analyze, not as research findings. The page shows no date.
