## Questions the results raise

- The word "panic" in these papers sometimes names something they were trying to *stop*, as in the *Inquirer*'s October 8 headline. It's worth thinking about what "panic" means in your position: the tone of the coverage, or the public mood the papers were reacting to.
- On the Michigan essay's account, the *Inquirer* and the *Bulletin* disagreed about the closures. Your position may need to treat "the papers" as several voices, not one.
- Even at the October 7 peak, the *Ledger* was printing reassurance next to the alarming numbers. That bears on the timing of any shift.
