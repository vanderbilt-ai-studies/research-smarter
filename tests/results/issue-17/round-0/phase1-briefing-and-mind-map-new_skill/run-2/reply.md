## Briefing: four-day school weeks

**Key debates**
- **Learning.** Results range from small gains to small losses. A 2021 Oregon study in the *Journal of Public Economics* found modest drops in math and reading ([Thompson, IZA working-paper version](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/12204)). A 12-state study found losses mainly in districts that cut total school time the most ([Thompson & Ward, *Economics of Education Review*, 2022](https://www.russellsage.org/node/4796)).
- **Teachers.** Districts often use the schedule as a hiring perk, but studies disagree. A Texas study found teacher turnover fell about 2.7 percentage points, though more than 1 in 5 teachers still left each year ([*Education Week*, 2026](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/do-4-day-school-weeks-attract-and-retain-better-teachers-what-the-largest-study-yet-says/2026/01)). Search results describe an Oregon study where turnover went up instead. I didn't open that one.
- **The day off.** A Colorado study found more youth crime after districts switched (Fischer & Argyle, *Economics of Education Review*, 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.econedurev.2018.03.010; I confirmed it in Crossref but couldn't open the article). An Oregon survey study linked the schedule to more food insecurity and drug use ([Tomayko et al., *Journal of School Health*, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8595551)). A multi-state RAND study did not find more food insecurity.

**Major voices**
- Paul Thompson (Oregon State) appears on many of the achievement and health studies.
- RAND produced the largest mixed-methods study: [*Does Four Equal Five?* (2021)](https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/does-four-equal-five-implementation-and-outcomes-of-the-four-day-school-week/).
- The [National Conference of State Legislatures](https://www.ncsl.org/education/four-day-school-week-overview) tracks how many districts use the schedule. As of 2023 it counted about 850 districts in 24 states, up from 650 in 2020.

**Patterns**
- Most strong studies are by economists comparing test scores before and after a district switches.
- Results differ a lot by state, and most four-day districts are small and rural.
- Measured cost savings are small. NCSL cites 0.4% to 2.5% of budgets.
- People's satisfaction often runs higher than the measured benefits.

**Open questions**
- How the schedule affects working parents and childcare has mostly been studied through focus groups, not large data sets.
- Older students and long-term outcomes like graduation get less attention.
- Larger, non-rural districts that switched recently are barely covered.
- A newer crime study, published in the *Journal of Policy Analysis and Management* in 2026, is out. I found it only as a Crossref record and haven't read it.
